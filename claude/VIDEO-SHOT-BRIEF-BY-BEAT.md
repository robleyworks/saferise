# VIDEO — WHAT THE PICTURE MUST REPRESENT, BEAT BY BEAT

23 September. Both films, every shot, in script order.

Companion to `claude/VIDEO-FRAME-MANIFEST.md` (what exists and qualifies) and
`claude/VIDEO-RESOLUTION-RULING.md` (the x1.31 rule). This one says what each
picture has to **mean** where it sits — so a candidate can be judged against
intent rather than against a filename.

**B2B is v10**, not the v9 list in `VIDEO-SCREENPLAY-PROVISIONING.md`. Three
beats are new and the boardroom moved. Timings are at 2.30 w/s, the recommended
pace — still unresolved, so treat positions as relative, not as cut points.

---

# FILM 1 · B2C homepage — 14 shots, 2:02

### 1 · 0:00 · the thesis, before any evidence *(20s — the longest hold in either film)*

> *The same conversation lands one way when you are calm, and another way when you are anxious. · Nothing about the conversation changed. · Your state decided how you were going to react before you did.*

**Represents:** nothing, deliberately. An abstract idea is being placed before
the viewer has been given any reason to accept it, and the picture's whole job
is to be a surface for attention rather than a subject of it. Near-black, one
edge of window light. Afterwards the viewer should not be able to say what they
were looking at.

**Fails if:** it has a subject, a face, an event, or anything readable. Anything
that raises a question takes the twenty seconds away from the sentence.

**Motion:** the most headroom in the film. A slow push, never a cut.

---

### 2 · 0:19 · the first of four states

> *Three in the morning. Awake with dread, and nothing to point it at.*

**Represents:** awake when nobody chose to be. Dread as distinct from sadness
and from fear of a thing — it has no object, which is exactly what makes it
hard to describe and why the picture carries it. The read is stillness with the
eyes open.

**Fails if:** asleep, peaceful, or smiling. A visible clock — the product rule
extends to the film. Overhead light; this is lit low and from the side.

---

### 3 · 0:26 · anger

> *The tone you used on someone you love, because the anger arrived before you did.*

**Represents:** being behind your own reaction. A domestic interior, two people,
and the moment *after* the tone landed rather than the argument itself. The line
is about arriving late to your own behaviour.

**Fails if:** it reads as violence or abuse rather than a bad moment. Fails if
anyone looks reconciled — resolution here undoes the next eight beats.

**Source:** graded, `b2c-triptych-strain` panel 1 — blocked on resolution, and
the file is not on disk.

---

### 4 · 0:34 · shame

> *The message you left unopened for a week, because opening it meant feeling the shame again.*

**Represents:** avoidance as a physical posture. Shame is the state; the
unopened message is only its evidence. The person is *with* the object and not
acting on it — proximity without contact.

**Fails if:** the screen is readable. Fails if it reaches for tears or despair,
which are bigger than the line and make it melodrama.

**Source:** graded, `b2c-triptych-strain` panel 3 — blocked, not on disk.

---

### 5 · 0:43 · panic

> *The meeting where the panic took everything you knew with it.*

**Represents:** a meeting seen from outside it. Panic narrows the person's own
view, so the camera stands off — from a doorway, over a shoulder, through glass.
Competence is present in the room and unavailable to one person in it.

**Fails if:** any face is readable. Fails if it dramatises; the line already has
the drama and the picture should be flat by comparison.

**Source:** to generate (B2C-R1). Test `method/m-outward` at x1.20 first — the
seated backs may satisfy the no-face rule and save the spend.

---

### 6 · 0:49 · recognition

> *Dread. Anger. Shame. Panic.*

**Represents:** the four arriving as one thing. Four returns to shots 2-5 at
roughly half a second each. The speed is the point — this is the viewer
recognising a pattern, not reviewing four examples.

**Source:** edit only, no asset.

---

### 7 · 0:52 · the turn

> *Those are not four problems. That is one system, in four states.*

**Represents:** a full stop. After four fast returns the picture has to stop
dead so the sentence can land. Its job is resolution, not information — this is
the moment the film's argument actually turns.

**Fails if:** anything moves, anyone is in it, or any texture pulls the eye.

---

### 8 · 0:58 · capability, elsewhere

> *You have had good judgement before. You have handled harder things than this.*

**Represents:** capability that is not currently in the room. Somewhere the
viewer moves through competently every day and is not in right now — a corridor,
a stairwell, a threshold. The **absence** of a person is what makes it about
them.

**Fails if:** it reads institutional. This is the consumer film and the viewer
must not place the frame at work. Fails if anyone is in it.

**No qualifying frame.** The nominated corridor is x1.35.

---

### 9 · 1:06 · and not reachable

> *None of that left. In certain states you cannot reach that part of yourself.*

**Represents:** the same place, one step in. Continuity is the entire point — if
it reads as a new location the cut becomes a new scene and the sentence loses
its subject. Cutting in on the same picture is the correct technique here.

Its one candidate stands alone rather than pairing with shot 8, so the
continuity breaks unless shot 8 is re-sourced to match it.

---

### 10 · 1:13 · the cost, landing on someone else

> *And the people around you met whichever state arrived.*

**Represents:** the state in the room, received by other people. Four at a
table. The subject is not the centre of attention — the others are, because the
line is about what they had to absorb.

**Source:** graded, `b2c-table-warm` — blocked, not on disk.

---

### 11 · 1:18 · the mechanism

> *A nervous system decides that, and it decides first.*

**Represents:** the mechanism personified without being explained. A face turned
away, half-lit by a window, doing nothing. **The one shot that must breathe** —
a single real movement, a breath or a blink, is the whole reason it was scoped
as motion rather than a still.

**Fails if:** the face is identifiable or the expression readable.

**Better route:** `galaxy/t1-04-clear` already *is* this picture at native
1920x1080. Animate that rather than generating a new one.

---

### 12 · 1:23 · what SafeRise does

> *SafeRise works on the state, not the situation. · Recognise what is running. Regulate it. Get your judgement back, and make the choice you would have made anyway.*

**Represents:** ordinary, present, unremarkable. A hand, a table, a window,
plain light. The first frame in the film that is not under strain — and the
relief has to come from the ordinariness, not from anything uplifting.

**Fails if:** a phone, laptop, headphones, app or logo is anywhere in it. The
beat is undone by a device, because the argument is that this works on the
state — not that it is an application. Fails if beatific.

**No qualifying frame.** The nearest repo candidate has a laptop in it.

**Note:** at 15s this is the longest block in the film and it carries four
separate claims. Worth considering whether it should breathe across two frames.

---

### 13 · 1:38 · what changes

> *One session is where it begins. · It changes the state you are in — which changes what you can do next.*

**Represents:** a morning and a door. Someone going to the thing they have to do
anyway. Motion outward, deliberately small — the line is modest and the picture
has to be modest with it.

**Fails if:** triumphant. No sunrise, no summit, no arms out. Fails if the
person reads as transformed rather than as functioning.

**Line change, 23 September.** The original opener — *"One session will not
rearrange your life"* — was rejected: it leads with a negation of a claim nobody
made, and it denies the wrong thing, since the film's axis is state, not
life-rearrangement. Beat 12 already carries "works on the state, not the
situation", so beat 13 must advance rather than re-argue. Options offered:

1. *One session is where it begins.* (recommended — four-word swap, cadence intact)
2. *One session begins it. Repeat it, and the range of what you can reach widens.*
3. *One session begins the rearranging. Not of your life — of what you are able to bring to it.*
4. *Start with one. The state shifts, and your choices come back with it.*
5. *One session changes the state you walk out in. That is where the rest starts.*

---

> *(six seconds, silent)* then *Get first access.* then *Start with the one you recognise.*

Covers and type. No photography. Blocked on cover remediation.

# FILM 2 · B2B organisations — v10, 19 beats, 2:38

### 1 · 0:00 · expertise, present *(held across beats 1-2)*

> *You hired these people for their expertise. You have seen what it looks like when they have it.*

**Represents:** a workplace before anyone arrives, at the start of a day.
Competence implied by the equipment and the order of the room, never
demonstrated by a person. The buyer should recognise their own operation in it.

**Fails if:** it reads abandoned or after-hours — the difference is entirely in
the light, and this is early, not late. Fails if it is generic open-plan office:
the first market is hospitality, healthcare and aviation, and the room should
say one of them.

**Motion:** held across two beats, so it needs headroom.

**No qualifying frame.** Nominated org band is x1.69.

---

### 2 · 0:07 · and not available

> *And there are hours when none of that expertise is available.*

**Represents:** the same room, unchanged, while the claim reverses. The picture
*not* changing is the argument — nothing about the workplace is wrong, which is
the whole premise the film rests on.

**Source:** hold on beat 1. Edit only.

---

### 3 · 0:12 · the handover

> *The handover after a bad shift, given while still shaking.*

**Represents:** mid-shift, a corridor or a station. Somebody who has just
finished something hard and is now required to speak clearly to the next person.
The strain sits in the body, not the face.

**Fails if:** anyone is smiling — this exact failure occurred in the first pass.
Fails if it reads as an emergency in progress; the shift is **over**, and that
is the point of the line. Fails on legible insignia or a facility brand.

**Proposed:** Adobe Stock 254790848. See `claude/VIDEO-DIVERSITY-CALL-SHEET.md`.

---

### 4 · 0:16 · the feedback

> *The feedback that turned into defensiveness before the second sentence was out.*

**Represents:** the moment listening stopped. Two people — one still talking,
one already elsewhere. Defensiveness as a posture, not an outburst.

**Fails if:** it becomes confrontation as spectacle. This is a working
interaction going quietly wrong.

**Source:** graded, `b2b-triptych-strain` panel 1 — blocked, not on disk.

---

### 5 · 0:22 · the decision

> *The decision taken while exhausted and resentful, by somebody who would have decided differently on a calmer day.*

**Represents:** depletion still on shift. Head down, others continuing behind.
The decision itself is not visible — the state that produced it is.

**Source:** graded, `b2b-triptych-strain` panel 2 — blocked, not on disk.

---

### 6 · 0:30 · the pivot

> *The training is not missing. Access to it is.*

**Represents:** nothing new. Seven words carry the film's whole thesis, and they
have just been earned by three concrete moments — so the picture must not
introduce anything. Hold on beat 5, or the quietest frame available.

**Changed in v10:** the boardroom moved off this beat to beat 7.

---

### 7 · 0:33 · the category SafeRise displaces

> *Frameworks and leadership models assume attention is available. SafeRise works one layer earlier — the state a person is in while the moment is happening.*

**Represents:** expertise on display in a room — the training itself, happening,
working. This is the only beat that shows what SafeRise sits underneath, and it
should look competent and well-run.

**Fails if:** it mocks the training. The argument is that it is **fine and
unreachable**, not that it is bad. A picture that sneers loses the L&D buyer in
the one beat written to reach them.

**Source:** `b2b-boardroom`, already graded — moved here from beat 6. Not on disk.

---

### 8 · 0:44 · the assistance programme

> *An assistance programme waits for someone to make the request, then routes them to a stranger. The solution arrives as exposure — a webinar, a portal, a library nobody opens.*

**Represents:** a room set up for a session nobody came to. Chairs in rows,
lights on, empty. The distinguishing detail is usually one chair out of line and
something left behind.

**Fails if:** it reads pre-session rather than unused — that is the single
hardest thing to get right here. Fails on any branding on the walls or screen.

**Proposed:** Adobe Stock 407957008.

---

### 9 · 0:57 · how nervous systems actually change

> *Adult nervous systems do not reorganise from exposure. They reorganise under attention, repeated.*

**Represents:** sustained attention as a working condition. A dispatch desk, a
control position, a monitoring station — somebody whose job is to keep
attending, hour after hour.

**Fails if:** it reads high-drama. This is the film's most abstract claim and it
needs the least dramatic picture in the film under it.

**Proposed:** Adobe Stock 545625176 (African) or 447052376 (European). Also the
first line to cut if the runtime has to come down — beat 10 now does its job
concretely.

---

### 10 · 1:03 · the product, for the first time

> *That is what a SafeRise protocol does. Name the state before it takes over. Work it down with breath and attention. Come back with room to judge what is actually there.*

**Represents:** the thing being funded. Three screen recordings of the protocol
player — at Recognise, at Regulate, and at the point where choice comes back.
A buyer has now watched a minute of argument without seeing the product at all,
and this beat exists to fix that.

**Constraint:** no durations, timers, countdowns, scrubbers or progress bars
anywhere in frame. The product rule holds on screen capture too.

**Blocked on:** the player UI being settled. These are the same captures the
member orientation film needs — shoot once, use twice.

---

### 11 · 1:16 · the modest claim

> *Pressure does not disappear. This is a repeatable way to meet it, without the most activated part of you making every decision.*

**Represents:** the claim staying small while the picture stays on the product.
Hold on the last of the three recordings. The hold is what keeps it from
sounding like a promise.

**Source:** edit only.

---

### 12 · 1:26 · everyone

> *Every employee. From the first day. No referral, no waiting list, no opening hours, and no limit on how many need it at once.*

**Represents:** the people an assistance programme never reaches. Back-of-house,
apron, kitchen, loading bay — working, not deciding.

**Fails if:** anyone is at a desk. The force of the line is that it covers the
people a portal does not. Fails if the work reads menial rather than skilled —
the film's argument is that these are the experts too.

**Proposed:** Adobe Stock 207772460 (European) or 451517772 (mixed, B&W caveat).

> *Everyone gets the foundation. Then one built for what they do, and one built for where they do it.*

Carousel — Foundation covers, then switching to Role and Industry. No

### 14 · 1:45 · not the same bad hour

> *A nurse on a ward, a teacher at three in the afternoon, and a front-desk supervisor in a resort do not have the same bad hour. They should not be handed the same session.*

Sector list builds beside the carousel. No photography. The three examples are

> *Talk to us about first access for your roles and your sectors.*

The list completes. No photography.

### 17 · 2:10 · the morning arriving at ten

> *A state that could have ended at six in the morning should not arrive in a client meeting at ten.*

**Represents:** two people at a table with a document — the meeting the
morning's state arrived at. The cost is being paid by both of them and neither
is discussing it.

**Source:** graded, `b2b-triptych-strain` panel 3 — blocked, not on disk.

---

### 18 · 2:18 · what the organisation is buying

> *You fund access. They own what happens inside it. What comes back to you is the hour, met by somebody who can reach their own judgement.*

**Represents:** an organisation as a set of lit windows — many people,
separately, at the end of a day. Dusk, mid-rise, from across a street. The
**separateness** is the privacy position made visual: the company funds the
building, not what happens in each room.

**Fails if:** branded. Fails if it is a glass tower — a corporate skyline
contradicts the first market and the argument the film just spent two minutes
making.

**Proposed:** Adobe Stock 539160375.

---

### 19 · 2:30 · the close

> *Start where it is worst. Let us show you what the SafeRise protocols would look like in your organisation.*

End card. No photography.
