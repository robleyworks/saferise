# Sovereign session — the screen

1 October 2026. Founder-ruled. Sits with `SOVEREIGN-ARCHITECTURE-DECISIONS.md`,
which covers the cloud/device split; this document covers what a member sees and
does during a voiced session.

---

## 1 · What this is

In a Guided session the member listens and the recording carries them. In a
Sovereign session **the member speaks and nothing is playing to carry them** —
so the screen has to do the work the voice does, without becoming an instructor.

Four things make that work: a sound bed under the session, a prompt on screen
for each phase, a way to move between phases, and a way to go back.

## 2 · The sound bed

**S1 · The bed is the one from that protocol's own guided meditation.** Closed
1 October — the handover landed and is verified in
`SOVEREIGN-SOUND-BED-DECISIONS.md`, which carries the protocol-to-asset mapping.
Twenty unique assets cover all thirty protocols. The path lives in the data
module, never in markup.

**S2 · It starts on the member's own tap to begin, not on page load.** Browsers
block audio without a user gesture, so autoplay is not available — and it should
not be. A bed arriving unannounced when someone is already activated, possibly
in a shared space, is its own problem.

**S3 · The member can turn it off, at any point, without leaving the session.**
Off stays off for that session. Whether the preference persists across sessions
is open.

**S4 · The bed plays as supplied, and it does not loop.** Founder-ruled
1 October. The files keep their 3 s fade in and 8 s fade out, and a bed shorter
than the session simply ends.

Recorded as a disagreement, because the rule it brushes against is load-bearing
elsewhere. These beds are cut to the recorded voice, so the fade-out arrives at a
fixed clock time in a session that has no clock, and a member still in Release at
9:52 hears the music go. Measured, the last six seconds of t1-07 fall to −36 dB,
so it reads as an ending rather than as the music thinning. That is a pacing
signal on a practice surface, which M1 and M2 otherwise refuse, and it is why the
alternative was to loop.

Overruled, and the build follows the ruling. **What the player must not do is
compound it: the end of the bed is not an event.** No auto-advance, no phase
change, no state change, nothing appearing or disappearing, no unprompted offer to
restart it, no console or UI acknowledgement. The music stops and the session
carries on exactly as it was. Whatever the audio does, nothing on screen marks it.

## 3 · The prompts

**P1 · One prompt per phase, derived from that protocol's own recording script.**
Not written fresh. Each prompt is a translation of what the guided voice says at
that step, from *"here is what is happening to you"* into *"here is what to
say"*, keeping the founder's language and order.

**P2 · The prompt stays on screen for the whole phase.** Founder-ruled. It does
not fade, advance, or reveal itself in parts. A member with their eyes closed
can open them at any point and find it still there.

**P3 · Regulate has little to say, and the prompt says so.** It is a breath
count. If the screen demands speech there, the member either forces something or
feels they have failed. The prompt states plainly that there is nothing to say.

### The source of the prompts

`SafeRise_AllTracks_RecordingScripts.docx` is the source, **not**
`SafeRise_SpokenTextOnly.md`. The spoken-text file strips booth apparatus by
design, including `RED/ACTION` cues — which are the somatic movement. Deriving
prompts from it loses the entire movement layer.

Where the movement actually sits:

| Phase | RED/ACTION cues | What they are |
|---|---|---|
| Regulate | 31 | Hand to sternum. Hands unclench — "fingers wide, then loose." Feet pressed into the floor, "feel the floor push back." |
| Rise | 6 | "Would your toes move?" · "Move your fingers. One hand. That's all this asks." |

These do different jobs. Regulate's settle the body. **Rise's are the smallest
possible voluntary act, which is how a person comes back from shutdown** — the
first move of authorship, which is why it belongs in Rise and not earlier.

Every cue in the scripts carries the founder's own stance: **"Offered, not
instructed."** The prompts keep that. Nothing on this screen is an order.

Humming on the exhale is **not** in the scripts. "Hum" appears only as a
sensation a member might notice ("a hum through the arms with nowhere to go").
Adding it would be new, not restored.

### The arc

Recognise is **attention** — deliberately placed rather than grabbed. Release is
**awareness** — seeing the brace as an old pattern rather than this room. Rise is
**authorship**. The member's own line: *attention becomes awareness, and
awareness becomes authorship.*

### The Clearing (T0-00) — approved, and the pattern for the rest

**Recognise**
> Start right in your body. Notice your jaw, your shoulders, the weight in the
> pit of your stomach — wherever you're holding on. Name what you find, quietly,
> without needing to change a thing.
>
> Now look one layer deeper: the emotion sitting on those sensations, and the
> thoughts spinning around them. Beneath the surface noise is what actually
> needs you. Give that root one simple word.

**Regulate**
> Nothing to solve right now. This is just breath.
>
> Inhale softly through your nose for four. Let it out, slow and complete, for
> six. Always a longer exhale than inhale.
>
> A hand over the centre of your chest, if you want one. Let your hands open —
> fingers wide, then loose. Feet into the floor, and feel the floor push back.
>
> Thank your heart for keeping you grounded here.

**Release**
> The tension you're feeling is answering an old memory, not the room you're
> actually sitting in.
>
> That physical brace was just an instinct trying to keep you safe. Gently name
> what your system has been bracing against — the old expectation, the fear, the
> past outcome.
>
> Now unhook today from that old backlog. You have full permission to drop what
> doesn't belong to this moment.

**Rise**
> Move your fingers. One hand. That's the whole ask — and it's yours.
>
> Now step outside it. Picture yourself over there, not in trouble — and speak
> to that person as you. Someone safe and capable.
>
> The Chosen Self: the version of you who meets this trigger next time. How they
> stand, how they breathe, how they answer with calm sovereignty instead of
> defence.
>
> Then what you're building — not the distant goal, the work right in front of
> you today. Where are your feet, what's in your hands, what's your very next
> move? Speak it out loud.
>
> And one good thing that's already here with you now.

**Two things these settle for every other protocol.**

The heart gratitude belongs in **Regulate**, not Rise. HeartMath's Quick
Coherence is heart focus, heart breathing, heart feeling — and the third is
appreciation. Regulate already had the first two and was missing the third,
while Rise carried it *and* "one good thing already here", so appreciation
appeared twice in the wrong step and not at all in the right one.

**Self-distancing is the active ingredient in Rise, not a tone.** "Speak to
yourself as someone safe and capable" is first-person self-talk and does not do
the work. The mechanic — Kross, whose framework page the product carries — is
addressing yourself as *you*, as someone over there. It was lost twice in
drafting and is restored here deliberately.

## 4 · Moving between phases

**M1 · The member advances.** One tap, or a spoken "next". No timer, no word
count, no readiness detection.

Three reasons, and the first is the one that matters: **deciding when a member
has recognised enough is a judgement SafeRise refuses to make everywhere else.**
Nothing is graded, nothing is diagnosed. Getting it wrong cuts someone off
mid-sentence while activated, which is the worst available moment to be
interrupted by software.

The other two are mechanical. Regulate produces almost no speech (P3), so there
is no signal to detect — automatic advance would fall back to a timer, and
timers are forbidden on a practice surface. And with the prompt persistent (P2),
nothing disappears to indicate the phase is over.

**M2 · No progress indicator of any kind.** No bar, no "2 of 4", no dots
filling. The standing rule, and it would also reintroduce the pacing M1 removes.

**M3 · A back button, to return to any phase already begun.**

**M4 · Going back APPENDS; it never replaces.** What the member said the first
time is kept, and the second pass is added to it. Never destroying a member's
own words is the promise the whole product rests on, and a back button that
silently overwrote a phase would break it at exactly the moment someone felt
they had not finished.

## 5 · What this does not change

Audio is discarded as it is transcribed. Transcription runs on the device. The
member's voice is never sent anywhere. AI feedback appears only after the member
has rated their own closing state, so it cannot colour that rating, and it is
descriptive and traceable — every line points at something they actually said.

On an employer-provisioned seat AI feedback is **off** until the employee turns
it on (B11), only they may turn it on or off, and the employer is never told
either way (B9).
