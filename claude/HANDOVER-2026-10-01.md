## 2 · Founder rulings made this session

**B4–B12 · B2B.** Two organisational plans (Premium and Sovereign licences),
annual seat pricing, a 30-day PoC rolling into a full-year licence, and AI
feedback **on by default for consumer Sovereign, off on employer-provisioned
seats** until the employee turns it on. Only the employee may switch it, and the
employer is never told either way. The switch must be prominent on login to an
organisation portal. Seat figures are recorded as **unratified**.

**S4 · The beds play as supplied and do not loop.** Fades intact; a bed shorter
than the session simply ends.

This one carries a recorded disagreement, kept in the spec rather than dropped,
because the rule it brushes against is load-bearing elsewhere. The beds are cut
to the recorded voice, so the fade-out arrives at a **fixed clock time in a
session that has no clock** — measured, the last six seconds of `t1-07` fall to
−36 dB, audible as an ending rather than as the music thinning. M1 and M2
otherwise refuse pacing signals on a practice surface.

Overruled, and the build follows the ruling. **The binding constraint that comes
with it: the end of the bed is not an event.** `ended` wired to nothing, no
auto-advance, no phase change, no state change, nothing appearing or
disappearing. The fade is already a signal; the screen must not confirm it.

**T0-00 keeps no Sovereign prompts.** The Clearing remains guided. Thirty
protocols carry prompts, not thirty-one.

**t1-03 ships the male take**, superseding the earlier one-voice ruling.

**t1-06 runs full at 17:37.** A 16:00 alternate exists but is a *different
recording*, not the 17:37 edited down — it differs from the installed master at
0:00, 1:00, 5:00 and 10:00. Not installed. Its matching 16:00 bed is unused.

**Beds are served same-origin, not from Supabase Storage.** `privacy.html` §4
calls which-protocol-you-opened sensitive, kept aggregated, never read person by
person. A bed fetched from Supabase would write a per-member, per-protocol,
timestamped record into the one account that also holds member identity.
Same-origin keeps it in the Netlify server logs already disclosed at 30-day
retention.

## 3 · Corrections found in supplied handovers

Three claims in the supplied audio handovers did not survive checking. Each
would have caused a wrong build.

**The bed dedupe was right; its stated method was not.** The handover said the
thirteen shared sessions were *"verified by checksum."* **All 31 files have
distinct MD5s** — `cmp` puts the first difference at byte 169, about 355 bytes
in a 14.4 MB file, which is the ID3 tag. Anyone re-running a checksum dedupe
concludes there are no duplicates and ships 434 MB. Verified properly by
comparing **decoded PCM**: three assets cover thirteen sessions.

Byte size is not evidence either — eleven files measure exactly **14407256
bytes** because 10:00 at 192 kbps CBR is that size whatever the music is, and
two different songs sit inside that group.

**`t1-03f` and `t1-03m` are not the same bed.** The handover lists both as "song
8". Different decoded audio.

**The hold on the last three meditations was wrong on two of three.** `t3-06` was
held as *"peak −0.4 dBFS, the hottest file in the library."* It is not: `t1-01`
is already installed at **−0.3** and `t3-03` at −1.0. The mastering chain was not
re-run.

| | I (LUFS) | LRA (LU) | True peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| t1-03 female (was) | −14.8 | **6.9** | −2.2 |
| t1-03 male (now) | −14.7 | **14.0** | −2.1 |
| t1-06 | −15.5 | 15.0 | −1.1 |
| t3-06 | −14.4 | 19.2 | −0.4 |
| t1-01 live | −14.7 | 19.1 | **−0.3** |
| t1-05 live | −14.6 | 16.8 | −2.2 |

The t1-03 female master's **LRA 6.9** was a third of the library's range —
caused by song 8 being a five-minute piece covering only the end of a
thirteen-minute session, leaving the first eight minutes as dry voice. The male
master measures 14.0 and its bed runs the full 13:02, so that gap is now closed
rather than documented.

**`t1-06` remains at −15.5 against a library of −14.2 to −14.8.** A +0.8 dB
re-encode was attempted and reverted: it produced only +0.5 on loudness and +0.2
on peak, landing at −15.0, still short. **That match belongs in the mastering
chain, not in a re-encode of an mp3.** Open.

## 5 · Netlify now meters credits — this changes the launch maths

The most consequential finding of the session, and it is commercial rather than
technical.

Netlify moved to **credit-based pricing**. A production deploy costs a flat **15
credits** regardless of size or build duration, and build minutes are no longer
metered. **Deploy size is not metered at all** — the 1.2 GB push cost exactly
what a one-line fix costs.

**Bandwidth is metered at 20 credits per GB**, and that is the problem.

| | Free | Personal | Pro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Credits / month | **300** | 1,000 | 3,000+ |
| Production deploys at 15 each | ~20 | ~66 | ~200 |
| *or* bandwidth at 20/GB | **~15 GB** | ~50 GB | ~150 GB |
| Overage | **none — sites pause** | purchasable | purchasable |

When credits run out on Free there is no overage: every site serves a *"Site not
available"* page until the cycle resets.

**The arithmetic.** A 17-minute meditation at 192 kbps is ~24 MB. A Sovereign bed
is ~14 MB. Fifteen GB is roughly **600 sessions a month** — and only if zero
credits go to deploys, which is impossible. Call it **400 real sessions** before
the site goes dark. That is about thirteen people practising daily.

**Four hundred sessions is not a launch.**

### What follows from it

The repo is now **1.2 GB tracked**, all of it uploaded on every deploy, with
`.git` at roughly 800 MB. Audio is 760 MB of that.

**Moving audio to Cloudflare R2 is now load-bearing rather than housekeeping.**
R2 charges nothing for egress. Pages and code are kilobytes; audio is megabytes,
so moving it removes almost the entire bandwidth line and leaves Netlify billing
only for deploys. The Cloudflare account exists — R2 returns *"enable R2 through
the dashboard"*, which is a toggle, not an auth failure.

`BEDS_BASE` and `MEDITATION_BASE` are each a single swap point, so the move is a
one-constant change per module and no markup edits.

The privacy position differs from the Supabase one ruled out in §2: a CDN on
your own subdomain never holds member identity, so there is nothing to join a
request to. It still needs a subprocessor row in `privacy.html` §7.

**Two unknowns:** which plan the account is on, and how many credits are spent.
Both are on the Netlify team billing page.

### Also worth recovering

`audit/` is **226 MB across 53 files** of screenshots — tracked, uploaded on
every deploy, and then `404!`'d at serve time by `_redirects`. A quarter of the
deploy payload is unreachable by design. Two single PNGs are 27.9 MB and 22.1 MB.
Removing it from the working tree stops future deploys carrying it, though
`.git` keeps it without a history rewrite.

## 8 · Standing constraints, unchanged

No borders anywhere — bezel, inset ring or shadow only. No durations, timers or
progress bars on any practice surface. Never state a protocol, resource or track
count in product copy, with the single exception of the plans page tier row.
Never use SpeechRecognition or any Web Speech API. Never put an API key value in
code, a comment, or any client-delivered file. Always update the privacy copy to
reflect the truth.

The three reconciled privacy claims: voice is never sent anywhere; typed content
stays on the device; a Sovereign record with AI feedback on is sent to Anthropic,
never used to train, and switchable off.

`SECRETS_SCAN_OMIT_KEYS` in `netlify.toml` lists values that are public by
design. **Never widen it to make a build pass.**

Git bus-errors on this repo over the Claude device bridge — `git status` exits
135 and `git add` can exit 0 having staged nothing. Run git from a normal
Terminal. Pushing needs GitHub Desktop or a terminal with credentials; the bridge
shell has none.
