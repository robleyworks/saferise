## THE CONSEQUENCE BEAT

The brief was to name what pressure costs — with discretion, and definitively.

> **"And you've seen where it lands. The mistake nobody can explain. The sick
> day that isn't. The good one who starts looking."**

**Why it is built this way.**

*Three artefacts, not four categories.* A manager does not experience "absence
rates"; they experience a specific Tuesday. Naming the artefact rather than the
category is what makes it discreet — it is observed, not measured — and it is
also what makes it definitive, because every people manager has seen all three
and knows there was no form to fill in for any of them.

*"The sick day that isn't"* says presenteeism and stress absence without saying
either, and without accusing anybody of lying.

*"The good one who starts looking"* is attrition, caught earlier than
resignation. It is more honest than naming a departure, because it describes
the stage a manager can actually see — and it avoids implying that SafeRise
prevents people leaving, which would be an outcome claim.

*"And you've seen where it lands"* keeps the whole beat inside the manager's own
experience. Nothing is asserted about the world; something is recalled.

**The ordering carries the governance.** The beat sits **before** "That's not a
training problem", so the sequence is **cost → diagnosis → SafeRise**, never
**cost → SafeRise.** The film names what pressure costs, then says the cost is
not a training gap, and only then introduces the product. If the product
arrived directly after the cost, the listener would hear a promise to reduce
it, which is the implied-outcome claim Track 03 forbids.

### ⛔ Leave "layoffs" out

It was in the brief and it should not be in the film, for two reasons. A layoff
is a decision the company makes, not something an employee's state produces —
so naming it alongside mistakes and sick days is simply inaccurate. And placing
it next to SafeRise implies the product protects jobs, which is the hardest
outcome claim in the set and the one most likely to be quoted back.

### Two shorter versions, if 1:46 is too long

| | Line | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| **Three beats** (recommended) | …The mistake nobody can explain. The sick day that isn't. The good one who starts looking. | 9.90s |
| **Two beats** | …The mistake nobody can explain. The good one who starts looking. | 7.62s |
| **One breath** | And you've seen where it lands — in the mistake, the sick day, the one who starts looking. | 6.40s |

The one-breath version is the most discreet and the least definitive; the three
beats are the version that stops a people manager scrolling.

### Picture: one shot, not three

Three images of misery cut together is the opposite of discretion, and it costs
three generations. **Play all three lines over a single held shot of an empty
desk** — a dark monitor, a chair pushed back, the floor still working behind it,
slow push, nothing else moving. One image carries all three meanings: the
mistake was made there, the absence is there, and the person who is looking has
not gone yet. It is also the cheapest thing in the whole extension to make.

## FLAGS

### 1 ⛔ "and that affects productivity" cannot be spoken

`B2B-ENTERPRISE-LAYER.md` §5 lists the words Track 03 forbids by its own rule:
**productivity · optimise · performance target · output · efficiency · high
performer · earning potential · best results · maximise** — plus no implied
outcomes. This film sells the organisational product that track sits under, so
the prohibition applies here more than anywhere.

That document also explains *why* the rule exists, and why keeping it is the
sale rather than a cost: *"A wellbeing product that declines to add a second
scoreboard. Leadership recognises the cynicism it answers."*

**Recommended replacement, which also sets up the next line better than the
original did:**

> "But you've seen pressure change what they can reach in the tough moments.
> **That's not a training problem.**"

Two alternatives that stay inside the rule:

- "…in the tough moments. And no amount of training reaches it."
- "…in the tough moments. That's the gap underneath everything you've bought."

The recommended version is one word longer than the original; the timings above
hold.

### 2 ✅ "Full organization access from day one" is correct — and is the strongest line in the script

`B2B-ENTERPRISE-LAYER.md` §2 rules exactly this: **"provision the whole
population by default, revoke on exit. Seats as an entitlement ledger, not as an
application process."** The reasoning is that *"an employee who has to request a
seat has disclosed something"* — the request is itself a signal, and the whole
proposition rests on nobody having to declare a difficulty to get help.

The same document's opening line for a first call is the one your sentence is
reaching for: *"Everything you have bought before required someone to raise
their hand. This doesn't."* Worth considering as the line itself.

**Commercial detail, if anyone asks after the film:** per-seat annual, 25-seat
minimum, banded 25–99 / 100–299 / 300–749 / 750+ scoped; Premium and Sovereign
licence plans; PoC 30 days converting to a full year.

### 3 ⚠ "Scoped to the industry and to their role" — is the content written?

Ruling **B2** in `B2B-PORTAL-DECISIONS.md`: *"Build the Industry and Role
protocol slots empty. The entitlement architecture, routing and portal
assignment are built now. Protocol content is uploaded into place when
written."*

The carousel shows thirty cards with IDs, scope names and anchors. If the
bodies behind them are still empty, the film is showing a catalogue an HR buyer
will go looking for. **Confirm the content status before this is shot.** If the
protocols are not written, the line holds as a roadmap statement only if the
film says so, which weakens it — better to shoot it when they exist.

### Also — the carousel still drifts two held sectors

The storyboard excludes **I12 Security Operations** on image grounds. It does
**not** exclude **I02 Emergency Response** or **R11 Protective Readiness**, which
sit under the same clinical hold and cannot appear until a clinician is
contracted. No number is spoken in this script, so removing them costs nothing.

## ONE LINE WORTH TIGHTENING

> "Extending their nervous system regulation not just to their state, but their
> responsibilities and environment."

Regulation is something a person does; responsibilities and an environment are
not things that get regulated, so the sentence asks the listener to do a small
piece of work mid-line. The meaning you want is that the *guidance* covers the
situation, not only the feeling. Closer:

> "Not just the state they're in — the room, the decision, the shift they have
> to finish."

Same length, same beat, and it lands on three concrete things an HR manager
recognises.
