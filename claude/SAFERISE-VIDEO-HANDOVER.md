### The hard constraints anything new must survive

- **No count of protocols, tracks, resources, roles or sectors** — spoken or on
  screen. This applies to the films. Investor documents are the exception.
- **No outcome promises.** Never *increases retention*, never *reduces
  absence*. Supports, may help, creates the conditions for.
- **Never imply regulation solves structural workplace problems.**
- **Nutrition and fitness are not sold as existing.** The safe formulation:
  *the same capability applies wherever a state is making the decision. We have
  built it for three areas of life so far, and we are building it for the rest.*
- **No readable text, screens, logos or branding** anywhere in any frame.
- **No durations, timers, countdowns, scrubbers or progress bars** on any
  practice surface.
- **These are life experiences people recognise, not diagnoses.**

## 2 · THE NARRATIVE SPINE

### B2C — the accumulation

Five people, each alone with a state, each telling what it cost them. Story one
opens **full frame**. On its last word it slides left and **freezes** into the
first fifth of the screen, holding a moment of distress, its title beneath it.
Story two enters and fills the remaining four fifths. And so on, until **five
frozen figures sit side by side** with every label visible.

The narrator turns on all five at once: *Those are not five problems. This is
one system, in five states.*

**Nothing keeps running after its story ends.** Each panel needs roughly 25
seconds of usable footage and one held frame.

### B2B — the cost, named to the buyer

An employee speaks in the first person. The narrator answers **immediately, and
answers to the buyer** — naming what that state cost the organisation in money,
people or reputation. The pattern runs four times, then the film turns to the
method.

The consequences are specific and none of them is a claim about SafeRise: a
resignation, a second period of sick leave, a public review, a client-facing
error, a notice sitting in a drafts folder. **The film never says SafeRise
prevents any of them.**

### Dashboard — the retention film

Instruction, not persuasion. It exists to stop somebody quitting in week four,
and it answers five objections before the member has them: *where do I start ·
the cue feels like a lesser version · I did it once and nothing happened · this
doesn't feel like me · I've lapsed so I've failed.*

The fourth is the week-four killer, and it cannot be delivered at the moment of
doubt — nobody watches a film then. It has to be planted early enough to be
remembered.

## 7 · ART DIRECTION AND CALL SHEET

### PART ONE · GLOBAL DIRECTION

#### 1 · The palette, and the one rule that protects it

The films live in the site's palette: near-black ground `#0A0A0F`, bone text
`#F5EDD8`, gold accent `#D4A843`, with sage, slate and a muted red as
secondaries.

> **Gold belongs to the light. Never to the wardrobe.**

Every warm highlight in these films — a lamp, low sun through a slat, a screen
glow — reads as the brand accent. The moment somebody wears gold, ochre or
mustard, the accent stops meaning anything. Keep clothing in the neutrals and
let the light carry the colour.

#### 2 · Wardrobe — the permitted range

| | |
|---|---|
| **Yes** | charcoal · near-black · bone · oatmeal · stone · deep olive · muted slate blue · faded rust, small areas only |
| **Fabric** | matte and absorbent — cotton, linen, wool, brushed knit, unwashed denim |
| **Fit** | slightly lived-in. A collar that has been worn all day, a sleeve pushed up |

**Never on camera, and each for a reason:**

- **Pure white** — blows out under a single warm source and the grade cannot
  recover it. Use bone or oatmeal, which read as white and hold detail.
- **Saturated blue or red** — fights the gold accent and drags the grade cold.
- **Fine stripes, checks, herringbone** — the classic generation artefact.
  Repeating high-frequency pattern is where these models visibly fail.
- **Logos, lanyards, ID badges, branded uniform** — the no-branding rule, and
  generated text is always garbled.
- **Shiny fabric, satin, leather sheen** — specular highlights read as a stock
  photo and blow under a warm key.
- **Glasses** — reflections plus rims are two independent artefact sources.
  Drop them unless a character needs one, and none do.
- **Watches with a readable face, statement jewellery** — small detail the
  model renders as mush.
- **Anything freshly pressed.** These people are at the end of something.

#### 3 · Grooming — found, not cast

Ordinary. Unstyled hair that has been through a day. **No visible makeup**,
no contouring, no gloss. Stubble where it is natural. A little shine on the
skin is correct — matte perfection reads as an advertisement, and the whole
film is an argument against advertisement.

**Faces should look like people you would not turn to look at.** The
moment somebody reads as a model, the viewer stops believing the story.

#### 4 · Light — one source, hard falloff

| | |
|---|---|
| **Sources** | one practical — a window, a lamp, a doorway. A second only as a weak fill |
| **Direction** | side or three-quarter back. **Never flat frontal** |
| **Falloff** | steep. Most of the frame should sit in shadow |
| **Colour** | warm interior, or cool daylight in the workplace scenes. Never mixed in the same frame |

**Never: overhead fluorescent.** It flattens the face, greens the skin, kills
the shadow the grade is built on, and it is the single most common failure in
workplace stock. Name it in the negative prompt every time.

#### 5 · Camera

| | |
|---|---|
| **Height** | eye level, seated subjects included. Never looking down on anyone |
| **Distance** | middle to close-middle. Head and shoulders plus room |
| **Lens feel** | 35–50mm. Shallow but not a portrait blur — the room must stay legible |
| **Movement** | locked, or the faintest handheld drift. **No push, no crane, no rack focus** |
| **Framing** | subject centred and upright, headroom above, **nothing load-bearing in the outer thirds** — every panel becomes a 384 × 1080 slice |

#### 6 · The universal never list

Readable text of any kind · screens showing anything · phones and laptops in
the strain scenes · earbuds and headphones · crowds · mirrors and reflective
surfaces · anyone crossing in front of the lead · anybody smiling unless the
script asks · crying · hands over the face · head in hands · slow motion ·
children's faces in focus · anyone looking into the lens.

### 3 · The register — one face, one object, one absence

**The person is in the scene of the issue.** Not observed from across a room,
not a portrait of a mood — inside the event the monologue describes.

But the expensive part of a scene is never the lead. It is **a second person
having to act.** A reaction shot is the hardest thing any of these models
renders: two faces, a timed exchange, an emotion that has to land on cue. That
is where the retakes go.

So the other party is present as **evidence, not performance**:

| instead of | use |
|---|---|
| a colleague reacting | a soft out-of-focus figure who never turns |
| someone handing him a page | **a hand entering frame** — no second face |
| a child watching and retreating | **a small backlit shape** in a doorway, stepping out |
| a manager mid-sentence | **the empty chair opposite**, and the letter already on the desk |
| a phone conversation | **the phone lighting up**, which is a light change, not a performance |

**One face has to work. Everything else is furniture, bokeh, or an object.**

This is not a retreat to the observational register. The scene is still the
event — the meeting is still happening, the daughter still came to the door, the
firing still happened. What changes is that the second party is shown by its
trace rather than by its acting.

#### The render rules that follow

- **Camera locked or barely drifting.** Handheld micro-drift is free; a move,
  a push or a rack focus is a retake generator.
- **Nobody crosses in front of the lead.** Traffic across frame is where
  limbs and faces break.
- **Never ask for an expression to change mid-clip.** Ask for a held state and
  **one physical beat** — a hand set down, a head turning a few degrees, a
  light going out.
- **Hands at middle distance, resting or doing one simple thing.** Hands in
  close-up doing detail work is the second-worst case after two faces.
- **No mouths speaking.** The voice is interior monologue; nothing needs to
  be said on camera, and speech is where faces go wrong.
- **Never in frame:** children's faces in focus, crowds, mirrors, reflections,
  readable text, screens showing anything.

#### Still true

**Faces are legible** — three-quarter, looking off, never locked to the lens.
**Compose vertical** — every panel ends up a 384 × 1080 slice, so the lead sits
centred with headroom and nothing load-bearing in the outer thirds.
**A panel is a scene cut into moments** — four clips, each a beat, chained from
the last frame of the one before.

### 8 · Where a still does the job instead of a clip

**The rule.** Generate video only where **a person must be alive in frame**, or
where **the motion is the argument**. Everywhere the subject is a room, an
object or an aftermath, generate a still and move it in the edit.

**The saving is not the still — it is that the move is free.** A 2% scale drift
or a slow drift across a still, done in the editor, costs nothing and looks
identical to a Runway clip that held almost motionless anyway. Paying 80 credits
for eight seconds of a room where nothing happens buys nothing but noise.

#### Swap these six clips for stills

| beat | was | why a still is enough |
|---|---|---|
| B2B 00 · before anyone arrives | 2 clips | Nobody is in it. The whole point is that nothing is happening yet |
| B2B 01b · the empty desk | 1 clip | Make the neighbouring desk empty too, and nothing needs to move |
| B2B 01b · the phone face down | 1 clip | An object on a counter |
| B2B 02b · the room after it ended | 1 clip | Two chairs, one pushed back. An aftermath |
| B2B 03b · the desk at end of day | 1 clip | Work stacked, chair empty, one lamp on |
| B2B 03b · the corridor, one light on | 1 clip | Nothing moves in it |

**Seven clips become seven stills.** 560 credits becomes 56 — and because an
image retake costs 2 credits against 80, the retake exposure on these
effectively disappears. Counting realistic iteration, this is **roughly 700
credits back**, or about 11% of the balance.

#### Where a still is actually better, not just cheaper

**An empty room on video draws attention to the fact that nothing is
happening.** Eight seconds of a still corridor with a faint camera wobble reads
as a mistake. The same frame held and drifted slowly reads as deliberate — as a
held look. For every aftermath beat in the B2B film, the still is the stronger
picture as well as the cheaper one.

#### Where video is not negotiable

- **All five character panels.** A person held for twenty-five seconds as a
  still is a photograph, and the film's whole claim is that these are living
  people in a state. This is what the budget is for.
- **B2C 11, the walk.** The motion *is* the meaning — one session is where it
  begins, movement outward.
- **B2B 04b, the feedback and the decision.** Both have a person close enough to
  read. A held still of somebody depleted looks posed; the involuntary stillness
  of a real hold is what sells it.

| **End card, each film** | Type over a still, not over motion |
| **Five held frames** | Already covered — pull from the panel clips, do not generate. They must match the clip exactly or the freeze jumps |

## 11 · DECISIONS ALREADY MADE, AND WHY

| decision | reason |
|---|---|
| **Voice is interior monologue, nobody speaks on camera** | No lip sync anywhere. Keeps casting a voice decision and the footage generatable |
| **Generate at 1080p, never upscale** | Upscaling costs more than twice generating natively |
| **Cast as stills first** | 2 credits against 80. This is what keeps the slate inside the balance |
| **Scenes, not documentary portraits** | A picture of a state is not the cost of a state, and the cost is what the script spent five rewrites reaching |
| **One face, one object, one absence** | A second person *acting* is the most expensive thing to render. The other party is present as evidence — a soft shape through glass, a hand entering frame, a silhouette leaving a doorway, an empty chair |
| **Three of five panels dual-purpose into B2B** | Both scripts put the same state in the same room. Five performers cover nine beats |
| **Stills replace seven B2B narrator clips** | The move is free in the edit, and an empty room on video draws attention to nothing happening |
| **Faces are legible now** | Nobody carries 25 seconds of first-person confession from the back of the head. But three-quarter and looking off, never locked to the lens |
| **Gold belongs to the light, never the wardrobe** | Every warm highlight reads as the brand accent. Wearing it destroys the accent |
| **No alcohol in the anger scene** | The instant a bottle appears the film is about something else |
| **B2B absorbs the operating-layer copy** | Beat 08 names the four steps as spoken; 08b restores *pressure doesn't disappear*, which stops the section sounding like a promise |

## 13 · WHAT TO BRING BACK

Whatever you develop elsewhere, these are the things this side needs in order
to keep going:

1. **Any change to a script line** — with the beat number, so runtime can be
   recomputed. The films are timed to the second and every change moves the
   total.

   the single object each scene turns on.

4. **Environment and wardrobe variations** you prefer, against the palette
   rules in §7.
5. **Anything you want added to the never list** — it is cheaper to exclude in
   the prompt than to retake.

### One caution for work in another tool

**Do not let anything rewrite the transcribed beats into polished copy.** B2C
00–08 and B2B 00–04b are spoken language and they are the reason the film
lands. A model asked to improve them will smooth out exactly the contractions,
repetitions and hesitations that make them sound like people.

If you want those beats worked on, ask for **alternatives alongside**, never
replacements in place.
