What every protocol opens

Ten resources, one for each kind of moment.

Not one recording. Ten pieces per protocol, and you use whichever the day calls for — all of them open from your first day. Each one says what it rests on, so you can see which part is evidence and which part is ours.

Guided Experience

The whole thing, carried for you.

The four steps voiced end to end, paced so you are not holding the sequence yourself while you are inside the state. Word for word, breath-marked, with the pauses built in rather than left to you.

Why voiced

A state narrows what you can hold in mind. Being talked through it removes the one job you cannot do well in that moment — remembering what comes next.

Rests on: Porges · Polyvagal Theory — peer-reviewed

Breathwork

Four in. Six out. The part that measurably works.

A paced rhythm at around six breaths a minute, which is where the gap between heartbeats becomes most even. Usable on its own, in a car park, without the rest of the protocol.

Why the long out-breath

Breathing out longer than you breathe in tips the balance toward the branch of your system that handles rest rather than threat. Your body reads the rhythm before your mind agrees to anything.

Rests on: HeartMath · cardiac coherence near 0.1 Hz — peer-reviewed

Somatic Release

For when sitting still is not available.

Something physical to do with the charge that is already in your body — pressure, movement, weight, temperature. For the states that will not be talked down and need discharging first.

Why the body first

Some activation has to go somewhere before attention is available at all. Asking someone to observe a state their body is still braced against is asking for the wrong thing in the wrong order.

Rests on: Porges · Polyvagal Theory — peer-reviewed

How This Works

The mechanism, not the metaphor.

What is actually happening in your body during this specific state, why these four steps land in this order, and what the research does and does not say about it.

Why explain it

Anti-dependency. If you understand the mechanism you can run it without us, adapt it, and eventually not need the recording at all. The aim is that you need this less, not more.

Rests on: All six frameworks, each with its register named

Protocol Guide

On paper. In your hand.

The printable step guide for this protocol — formatted for a phone screen, a wallet, or the fridge door. The whole sequence on one page with nothing to scroll.

Why printed

A device is a poor companion in the middle of a state. Something on paper does not notify you, does not need a signal, and does not offer you anything else to look at.

Rests on: SafeRise practice material · no external claim

Cue Card

Four lines. Always here.

You do not have to remember anything. Name it — finish the sentence. Hand to heart, in for four, out for six. Stop fighting it. One breath as the person on the other side.

Why four lines

Because the moment you need it is the moment you can hold least. This is the version for someone who has done it before and needs the order, not the explanation.

Rests on: Working-memory narrowing under threat — established

Progress Journal

Somewhere to put it down.

Prompts written for this specific protocol, and a place to write in your own words. Stays on your device. No sync, ever — including to us.

Why writing

Putting a state into your own sentences moves it from something you are inside to something you are looking at. That shift is the whole of the first step, done on paper.

Rests on: Maté · Compassionate Inquiry — clinical practice

Progress Tracking

What you noticed, before and after.

A record of the state you were in when you started and where you were when you finished. No score, no streak, no target, nothing to keep up or fall behind.

Why no score

Because you will arrive here on your worst day. A number that says you have slipped is the last thing that should meet you, and a number that says you are doing well is not why you came.

Rests on: SafeRise measurement · not an external claim

Attention Advisory

Where it belongs today. Which fights are not yours.

Guidance on where your attention actually needs to go in this pattern, and what it is being pulled toward that has nothing to offer you.

Why attention

Something you never consciously chose is deciding what receives your attention. Naming that, in this specific pattern, is where authorship starts.

Rests on: Scope boundary · reviewed with Dr Keny F. Bastien MD

Disclosure & Invitation

Words for the people around you.

A script for explaining what you are doing, and a structured way to open repair when the pattern involves someone else.

Why scripts

The hardest part is rarely the protocol. It is the first sentence to another person.

Rests on: SafeRise practice material · no external claim
