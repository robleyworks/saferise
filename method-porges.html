01What the theory says

Safety is decided before thought

The autonomic nervous system runs the parts of you that you do not operate by hand — heart rate, breathing, digestion, the tone of your face and voice. Porges's central proposal is that this system is continuously scanning for danger and for safety, and that it does so faster than conscious appraisal and without asking you. He named that scanning neuroception.

The second proposal is that the system does not simply run hot or cold. It has a small number of distinct states, and it moves between them in a predictable order. Each state was useful at some point. None of them is a malfunction.

The third proposal is the one that makes the theory clinically useful: the state you are in determines what is available to you. Reasoning, warmth, patience and perspective are functions of one state. When you are in another, they are not weakened — they are largely offline. This is why being told to calm down has never calmed anybody down, and why insight arrives after regulation rather than causing it.

You cannot talk a system out of a state it entered without consulting you. The working sentence behind Step 01

The last piece is co-regulation. Cues from other people — a face that is not braced, an unhurried voice, a steady presence — are read by the same machinery and register as evidence of safety. Human nervous systems settle in company more readily than alone. That is the whole argument for the workshops, and the reason a recorded voice is doing work that text cannot do.

AvailableSafe and social Settled enough to think, listen, be touched, change your mind. Not a mood — a physiological condition that makes those things possible.
ChargedMobilised Energy for action. Anxiety, urgency, irritation, racing thought. The system has decided something must be dealt with and has funded it.
WithdrawnShut down Flat, numb, heavy, far away. The oldest protection there is: when neither fighting nor fleeing will work, conserve. Not laziness and not depression.

Recovery runs through the middle. Coming up out of shutdown means passing through mobilisation, which is why the first honest sign of movement is often anger or agitation rather than calm.

02What SafeRise takes

Name it before you touch it

If threat detection runs ahead of thought, then the first useful move is not to argue, reframe or fix. It is to identify what state is actually present — because the state, not the story attached to it, is what determines the next few minutes. Every protocol therefore opens the same way.

Step 01

Identify & Name

Load-bearing. Naming the state specifically — mobilised, shut down, or somewhere between — replaces a verdict about yourself with a description of a condition. The three-state vocabulary comes directly from here.

Step 02

Redirect to Heart

Shared with HeartMath. Porges supplies the reason breath and posture reach the system at all; HeartMath supplies the specific rate. See the cardiac coherence page for the one empirical claim SafeRise makes.

Step 03

Accept & Release

Not this framework. Acceptance as a decision rather than a feeling comes from Compassionate Inquiry. Polyvagal theory describes the state; it does not tell you what to do about your relationship to it.

Step 04

Gratitude & Expansion

Not this framework. Rehearsing a future self is a separate practice with a separate and weaker evidence base, held in the interpretive layer.

04What we rest on

What the research gives us

What holds

Five findings the method is actually built on.

SafeRise uses polyvagal theory for what a person can notice and influence. Those are the parts with the firmest ground under them, and they are the parts the four steps are constructed from.

  • Threat and safety are registered faster than thought Detection runs ahead of conscious appraisal, which is why insight arrives after regulation rather than causing it — and why Step 01 names a state rather than arguing with it.
  • People move between recognisable states, and capacity moves with them Mobilised, settled, shut down. Reasoning, patience and perspective are functions of one of them. This is the vocabulary of the arrival check-in and the arc in Your record.
  • Breath and posture reach the system directly Slowing the breath and changing what the body is doing shifts state without going through argument. Step 02 and Somatic Release both exist because of this.
  • Withdrawal is protection, not failure The flat, distant, unreachable state is the oldest defence there is. The Shutdown Recovery Protocol is built on that reading, and so is the absence of any "good" end to the arc.
  • Other people regulate you A steady voice and an unhurried presence are read as evidence of safety. Co-regulation is why a recorded voice does work text cannot, and why the group workshops exist at all.

Polyvagal theory is also the subject of a live scientific dispute — a joint evaluation published in Clinical Neuropsychiatry in February 2026 challenges its anatomical and evolutionary premises, and Porges replied in the same issue. That argument is about mechanism: which brainstem structures do what, and how the arrangement evolved. The five findings above hold either way, and they are the ones the method runs on. Both papers are in the reading list below.

Read the arguments yourself

  • Porges, S. W. (1995). "Orienting in a defensive world: mammalian modifications of our evolutionary heritage. A Polyvagal Theory." Psychophysiology, 32(4), 301–318. The original statement, from his 1994 presidential address. doi:10.1111/j.1469-8986.1995.tb01213.x Primary source
  • Porges, S. W. (2011). The Polyvagal Theory. W. W. Norton. Book-length development of the theory, including neuroception and the three-state account. Primary source
  • Porges, S. W. (2022). "Polyvagal Theory: A Science of Safety." Frontiers in Integrative Neuroscience, 16, 871227. Shorter and more recent, and the better place to start. doi:10.3389/fnint.2022.871227 Primary source
  • Grossman, P. & Taylor, E. W. (2007). "Toward understanding respiratory sinus arrhythmia." Biological Psychology, 74(2), 263–285. The foundational objection to reading a vagal pathway off heart-rate variability. doi:10.1016/j.biopsycho.2005.11.014 Critique
  • Grossman, P. (2023). "Fundamental challenges and likely refutations of the five basic premises of the polyvagal theory." Biological Psychology, 180, 108589. Premise-by-premise challenge to the theory's anatomical and evolutionary claims. doi:10.1016/j.biopsycho.2023.108589 Critique
  • Grossman, P. et al. (2026). "Why the Polyvagal Theory Is Untenable." Clinical Neuropsychiatry, 23(1), 100–112. Thirty-nine specialists in vagal physiology and vertebrate evolution. The most serious challenge to date. doi:10.36131/cnfioritieditore20260110 Critique · 2026
  • Porges, S. W. (2026). "When a Critique Becomes Untenable: A Scholarly Response to Grossman et al." Clinical Neuropsychiatry, 23(1), 113–128. The rebuttal, in the same issue. Read it alongside the paper above, not instead of it. doi:10.36131/cnfioritieditore20260111 Reply · 2026
  • Dana, D. (2018). The Polyvagal Theory in Therapy. W. W. Norton. How the frame is used in practice, which is closer to how SafeRise uses it. Applied
