The method

Every protocol runs the same four steps, in this order. The sources beside them are what each step is built on.

Step 01 Identify & Name Name the state specifically, before doing anything to it.
Step 02 Redirect to Heart Hand to heart, about six breaths a minute, exhale longer than the inhale.
Step 03 Accept & Release Stop fighting what is there. Acceptance as a decision, not a feeling.
Step 04 Gratitude & Expansion One breath taken as the person on the other side of it.

Scroll to see which sources carry which step.

≈6breaths per minute

This is the only specific empirical claim SafeRise makes. Around six breaths a minute — 0.1 Hz — is where heart-rate variability peaks for most people. It is measurable, it has been replicated, and it is why Step 02 asks for a longer exhale than inhale.

It is the number Step 02 is built on: four seconds in, six seconds out, one cycle every ten seconds. Everything else here is framing, mechanism or practice, and labelled that way throughout.

Why guided meditation

Guided meditation is the delivery, and the reason it works

Every protocol is a guided meditation: one voice, a paced breath, and a sequence you follow with your eyes closed. That form was chosen because it is the only one that still works in the state you arrive in — and because a guided meditation does three things at once that no article, worksheet or screen prompt can do separately.

Reading is the thing you lost

When you are mobilised or shut down, the capacity to read carefully, hold instructions in mind and apply them in order is exactly what has gone offline. A worksheet asks for the one faculty the state has taken. Eyes closed and ears open is the channel still open to you.

A steady voice is itself a signal

Tone, pace and an unhurried delivery are read by the same system that reads threat, and they register as evidence of safety. A recorded voice does part of the settling before it has asked you to do anything. Text cannot do this. It is also why the group workshops exist.

Someone has to hold the tempo

Nobody reads an instruction and keeps a slow breathing rhythm at the same time — the counting takes the attention the breathing needs. The guide carries the pace so you don't have to track it, which is the whole reason the rate in Step 02 is reachable at all.

The music is a metronome you never have to count. Its phrases are built to the length of one breath cycle, so the tempo carries your breathing while your attention stays where the voice puts it. It works by rhythm, not by pitch.

Sessions vary in length by protocol and by track. Some are short enough to run before a difficult conversation; others are built to be sat with. What does not vary is the sequence and what it is for.

The practice buys you the state. What you do with the state is yours.

Noticing a pattern, choosing differently, holding your ground in a conversation that used to flatten you, becoming someone the people around you experience differently — every one of those is close to impossible in a mobilised or shut-down state, and available in a settled one. The session is how you get from one to the other, repeatably, without needing anyone else in the room.

A note from the founder

Why six, and not one

Every one of these frameworks is incomplete on its own, and each is incomplete in a way another one covers. Porges describes the state you are in but not what to do about your relationship to it. Maté answers that and says nothing about breath. HeartMath gives a rate that works and no account of why a person cannot simply decide to feel better. Watts describes the disposition all of it requires and offers no method at all. Picking one and building a product on it would have meant pretending the gaps were not there.

What SafeRise adds is the order. Name the state before you touch it, because you cannot work on something you have not identified. Settle the body before the mind, because the reasoning you need is not available until you do. Accept before releasing, because fighting a state is most of what sustains it. Then, and only then, look forward. The sequence is the contribution. The evidence underneath it belongs to the people named above, and the pages tell you exactly how far each of them reaches.
