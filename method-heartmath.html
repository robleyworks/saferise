01What the research says

The heart does not beat like a metronome

A healthy heart never beats at a fixed interval. The gap between beats lengthens and shortens continuously, and the size of that variation is called heart-rate variability. More variation generally indicates a system that can adapt. Less indicates one that is braced.

Breathing changes that pattern directly. You do not have to intend it and you cannot argue your way into it. Inhale and the interval shortens; exhale and it lengthens. At most breathing rates the resulting pattern is irregular. At roughly six breaths a minute it becomes a smooth, repeating wave — heart rhythm and breath moving together in a single oscillation. HeartMath calls this state coherence.

The rate is not arbitrary. Six breaths a minute sits near the frequency at which the body's own blood-pressure regulation already oscillates, so breathing there meets a rhythm the system is running anyway rather than imposing one. This is the least contested finding on this page, and it is the one the method uses.

What follows from it is contested. HeartMath's wider programme attaches broader claims to the coherent state — about emotion, cognition and interpersonal effects. SafeRise does not carry those claims. Section 04 says exactly where the line is.

The findingRoughly six breaths a minute Ten seconds a cycle. At this rate heart rhythm and breath lock into a single smooth wave. Replicated widely across independent laboratories.
The reasonIt meets an existing rhythm The body's blood-pressure regulation already oscillates near this frequency. You are joining something, not imposing something.
The limitA measurement, not a meaning What the coherent state signifies about emotion or cognition is a separate and much weaker set of claims. SafeRise does not carry them.

The count is uneven on purpose. The exhale is where the interval between beats lengthens, so six out matters more than four in — and if the numbers are uncomfortable, keep the ratio and shorten both.

02What SafeRise takes

One rate, and the reason it is that rate

Porges explains why breath reaches the system at all. HeartMath supplies the specific number. Step 02 asks for four counts in and six counts out, which lands close to six breaths a minute — and the longer exhale is doing the work, because that is the half of the cycle where the interval lengthens.

04What we rest on

One claim, and where it stops

What holds

Three findings hold. The rest of the programme is not ours.

This is the narrowest framework on the platform and the one where the gap between the replicated finding and the surrounding programme is widest. SafeRise takes the finding. It does not take the programme.

  • Slow breathing changes heart rhythm, reliably Around six breaths a minute the beat-to-beat pattern becomes a smooth oscillation. This replicates across independent laboratories. Step 02 is built on it.
  • The rate meets a rhythm the body already runs Six breaths a minute sits near the frequency of the body's own blood-pressure regulation, which is why that number and not another. Four in and six out is the practical form of it.
  • The exhale is the half that does the work Lengthening the out-breath is what extends the interval between beats. This is why every protocol asks for an uneven count rather than an even one.

Beyond those three, HeartMath's programme makes considerably larger claims — about emotional states, cognitive performance, and effects between people. SafeRise makes none of them. Step 02 asks you to breathe at a rate and says nothing about what the rhythm signifies. That is not a comment on their work; it is a statement of where this platform's claim ends.
