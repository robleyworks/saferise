01What the method says

The response made sense when it started

Compassionate Inquiry is not a theory about the nervous system. It is a way of asking a question, and the claim underneath it is that the register you ask in decides what you are able to find.

Maté's central move is to treat a symptom as an adaptation rather than a defect. The numbing, the vigilance, the compulsive achieving, the inability to be still — each of these did something useful once, usually early, usually in a situation the person did not choose. Asking why someone is like this produces a verdict. Asking what the behaviour was for produces information.

The second move is about self-address. Most people investigate themselves in a voice they would never use on anyone else. Maté's position is that this voice is not neutral — that a hostile interrogation reliably produces defensiveness or shutdown, in yourself exactly as it would in someone else, and that what is actually there stays hidden while you are still arguing with it.

The third move is the boundary. Compassion here is not agreement and not approval. You can see clearly why something started and still decide it is finished. Understanding the origin is what makes putting it down possible; it is not a licence to keep it.

The verdictWhat is wrong with me? Produces a judgement and nothing else. It assumes a defect, so the only available answers are about your character. Nothing here can be worked with.
The inquiryWhat was this for? Produces information. It assumes a function, so the answers are about a situation and a response — both of which are specific, and one of which has changed.
The boundaryAnd is it still needed? Understanding is not endorsement. Seeing why something began is what makes it possible to decide it is over.

The order matters. The third question is unanswerable while you are still in the first — you cannot retire a defence you have not understood, and you cannot understand one you are busy prosecuting.

02What SafeRise takes

Acceptance is a decision, not a feeling

Step 03 is the one people misread. It does not ask you to feel accepting, or to be at peace with anything. It asks you to stop spending force on the fact that this is happening, because the resistance is a second problem sitting on top of the first. That is a decision you can make while still disliking the thing entirely.

Step 01

Identify & Name

Shared with polyvagal theory. Porges supplies the vocabulary of states; this framework supplies the register — naming without a verdict attached. Both are needed or the step becomes self-assessment.

Step 02

Redirect to Heart

Not this framework. Breathing rate is physiology and comes from the cardiac coherence work. Nothing here says anything about how to breathe.

Step 03

Accept & Release

Load-bearing, and the whole reason this framework is on the platform. Ask what the response was for, in a register that does not prosecute — then decide separately whether it is still needed. Shared at the edge with non-resistance.

Step 04

Gratitude & Expansion

Not this framework. What comes after release is a separate practice. This one ends where the understanding lands; it makes no claim about what you build next.

04What we rest on

A practice, not a finding

What this is

Three things this framework gives the method — and one it does not.

This page sits in a different band from the two before it. Polyvagal theory and cardiac coherence are research programmes with experimental literatures, contested or otherwise. Compassionate Inquiry is a clinical practice — a method of asking, taught to practitioners, refined by use rather than by trial. It has no controlled evidence base of its own, and this page does not pretend otherwise.

  • A symptom is treated as an adaptation The response did something once. That reading is what makes Step 03 possible at all, because you cannot release something you are still trying to convict.
  • The register of the question decides the answer "What is wrong with me" and "what was this for" reach different material. Every prompt on this platform is written in the second register, and that is a direct inheritance.
  • Understanding is not endorsement Seeing why a pattern began is separable from deciding to keep it. This is the boundary that stops acceptance collapsing into resignation.
  • What it does not give us: a claim about outcomes Nothing here licenses a statement about what this practice will do for you, how long it takes, or what it treats. Where the platform makes no promises, this is partly why.

One boundary worth stating plainly. Maté's wider work reaches into questions of cause — what early experience does to later health. SafeRise makes no aetiological claim of any kind. The platform never tells you what caused anything, because that is not something a protocol can know and not something it needs to know in order to be useful.

The second is about scope. Compassionate Inquiry as Maté teaches it happens with a practitioner in the room, who can see what is surfacing and slow it down. A self-guided protocol has no such person. What SafeRise takes is the register and the shape of the question — not the depth of the clinical process, which is why Safe Practice exists and why the advisory sits on the protocols that reach earliest.

05Read it yourself

Read the arguments yourself

  • Maté, G. (2003). When the Body Says No: The Cost of Hidden Stress. Knopf Canada. Where the adaptation reading is set out at length. Primary source
  • Maté, G. (2008). In the Realm of Hungry Ghosts: Close Encounters with Addiction. Knopf Canada. The clearest statement of asking what a behaviour was for rather than what is wrong with the person doing it. Primary source
  • Maté, G. & Maté, D. (2022). The Myth of Normal. Avery. The most recent and the most expansive statement of the whole position. Primary source
  • Compassionate Inquiry (2019–). Professional training programme. The method as taught, including its scope and its supervision requirements. Useful for seeing what a self-guided practice cannot include. Applied
  • Felitti, V. J. et al. (1998). "Relationship of childhood abuse and household dysfunction to many of the leading causes of death in adults." American Journal of Preventive Medicine, 14(4), 245–258. The ACE study, which much of this work rests on. The ACE study's own author on how far its findings reach. The clearest statement of why this platform makes no claim about cause. doi:10.1016/j.amepre.2020.01.009 Where the claim stops
