# Market Intel — Sound Frequency / Somatic Release Segment

Logged 6 September 2026.
Source: competitor intro audio for a 14-day sound-based challenge, captured from a public
marketing page. Transcribed and analysed; not reproduced here in full.

---

## What the segment sells

A guided audio programme in which **sound itself is the active ingredient.** The listener
receives; they are not asked to do anything.

The mechanism is stated as recognition — the nervous system is said to respond to something
in the frequencies, as though the body had been waiting for a particular signal. The
promise attached to it is explicit: you do not have to do anything to make it work, you
just have to show up and listen.

That last sentence is the product.

---

## Structural observations

**Format.** Fourteen days, one session each morning, three named things the listener will
learn. Challenge structure with a fixed end date. Worth noting as a format independent of
the claims wrapped around it — a bounded commitment converts better than an open one, and
the daily-morning cadence does the retention work.

**Language.** "Practice" used as the product noun throughout, which SafeRise prohibits.
Their usage is a reminder of why the prohibition exists — repeated enough, the word turns
the thing into homework.

**Founder disclosure.** The strongest moment in the whole piece is the founder saying they
have been in these states for a long time and sometimes still are. Present tense. Most
operators in this category will not say that, because it reads as unresolved. It is the one
element worth learning from directly.

---

## Where the claim fails

Audible pitch has no established therapeutic property. The specific-frequency claims that
circulate in this segment — 528 Hz and the rest of the solfeggio set — trace to a
numerological reconstruction from the 1970s, not to any historical or clinical source. The
432 Hz tuning literature is small and mixed.

The structural problem is bigger than any individual claim. **A frequency cannot be
state-specific.** If a pitch heals, it heals identically whether the listener is agitated,
oscillating, or shut down. That leaves the whole segment unable to explain why its audio
would differ per state — and mostly it does not differ, which is the tell.

There is one adjacent claim that is real. Vibroacoustic therapy uses low frequencies,
roughly 30 to 120 Hz, delivered through the body as physical vibration in a chair or mat.
The evidence is modest but it exists. It is also a completely different delivery mechanism
from listening. Headphones are not transducers. Anyone citing vibroacoustic research in
support of a streamed audio product is right about the studies and wrong about what they
apply to.

---

## Their advantage

Passivity sells. "Just listen" is a lighter promise than "here is what you do," and it
converts better with an audience that has already failed at meditation and reads that
failure as personal. The mystical mechanism is not incidental to that — it is what licenses
the passivity. If sound does the work, the listener cannot do it wrong.

That is a real advantage and it should not be dismissed.

---

## Where SafeRise differs

The difference is not tone or production quality. It is who is doing the regulating.

Their listener is a **recipient.** The sound acts on them.
The SafeRise listener is an **agent.** The sound holds a pace; the breathing is theirs.

That is a harder sell and a more defensible product. It also survives the obvious question.
Asked what the mechanism is, SafeRise can answer with extended exhale, vagal brake, and
resonance-frequency breathing, all of it published and checkable. The frequency segment
cannot answer without either citing something that does not hold or going vague.

**The exposure runs the other way too.** SafeRise cites Porges and Kross by name. A platform
that names its sources gets audited against them. One decorative claim about healing
frequencies would make the real citations look like set dressing, and that damage is not
proportional to the size of the claim.

---

## Commercial note

Merchant-of-record providers apply acceptable-use policies to wellness claims, and specific
healing-mechanism assertions are the category that draws attention. Sellers making them
carry a risk that sellers describing rhythm and breathing do not. The honest position is
also the lower-risk one for the payment rail, which is a convenient alignment rather than
the reason for it.
