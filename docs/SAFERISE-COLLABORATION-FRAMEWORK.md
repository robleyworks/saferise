# SafeRise — Collaboration Operating Framework

**Version 1 · 8 September 2026 · Kenor International B.V.**

The standing structure for every collaborator, contributor, reviewer and adviser
who touches SafeRise Protocol. Written once, applied to everyone.

**Why this exists.** Two people have already turned out to be scoped wrong — one
credential taken on trust and later removed from the site, one role built on an
AI-generated document that attributed a qualification the person does not hold.
Neither was anyone's bad faith. Both were the absence of a structure.

---

## 1 · The four roles

Non-overlapping by design. None can be performed by the other three.

| Role | Holds | Never holds |
|---|---|---|
| **Founder** | Architecture, voice, protocol selection, what ships and when, all commercial terms | — |
| **Contributor** | Substantiation, sourcing, domain material, population accuracy | Clinical sign-off, contraindications, safety review |
| **Clinician** | Safety. Escalation criteria, exclusion criteria, scope statement | Voice, architecture, editorial control |
| **Lived-experience reader** | Whether it lands | Whether it is safe |

**The founder role does not delegate.** Everything upstream of substantiation
stays with Andre — it is the largest share of the work, it costs nothing but
time, and it is the part that cannot be handed over without the material ceasing
to sound like SafeRise.

**A contributor is not a junior clinician.** Someone with deep domain knowledge
and no licence is genuinely valuable and genuinely not a safety reviewer. The
structure must not stretch them past what they can carry, and most people will
scope themselves accurately if asked directly.

---

## 2 · Credential verification — the rule that was skipped

**Never research a collaborator from the open web.**

1. **Credentials come from the person, in writing.** Their own words, their own
   account of what they hold.
2. **Verified by Andre against the issuing register's own site.** Not a
   directory, not an aggregator, not their website, and never an AI summary.
3. **A licence means a number.** If someone describes themselves as licensed or
   registered and there is no number, that is not a formality gap — it means
   something different from what the phrase implies. Ask.
4. **No inference.** If a fact is not stated by the person or confirmed on a
   register, it does not exist. An institution's course catalogue is not evidence
   of what someone studied.

**Any AI-generated profile of a person is inadmissible.** Not as a starting
point, not as a draft to verify. The failure mode is confident specificity —
practicum hours, curriculum detail, licensure alignment — assembled from what a
programme *usually* contains rather than what this person actually did.

## 3 · Public description — the exact-words rule

**Every collaborator's public description is agreed word for word before it
appears anywhere.**

Titles are not descriptive language. Several are legally protected, and the
protection varies by jurisdiction — a term that is ordinary in one place is a
restricted title in another.

**The standing test:** does this wording state a credential the person holds, in
terms the issuing body would recognise?

- ✅ *"Certified in Functional Family Therapy"* — states the certification
- ❌ *"Functional Family Therapist"* — reads as a licensed title

Same distinction applies to *therapist*, *counsellor*, *psychologist*,
*clinician*, *doctor*, *practitioner*, *coach* and *specialist*. When uncertain,
describe the qualification rather than name the profession.

**Both directions.** The MOU binds what SafeRise may claim about them, and what
they may claim about SafeRise.

---

## 4 · Liability does not move

**Sourcing expertise through a collaborator does not transfer risk.**

Kenor International B.V. publishes it. Kenor carries it. A referral chain buys
better content and better reviewers — it is not a liability structure, and
treating it as one is how a company ends up with the exposure and none of the
protection.

**The only thing that reduces exposure is a contracted, licensed reviewer with a
defined scope, engaged directly by Kenor.**

⚠ **Every clinician contracts to Kenor, never through a collaborator.** A
contributor may find and brief them; the engagement letter, scope, payment and
sign-off are Andre's. A relationship that runs entirely through an intermediary
dissolves the moment that person's health, priorities or circumstances shift —
and then there is no clinical layer at all.

---

## 5 · Load, availability and the critical path

**Nobody unpaid sits on the critical path of anything with a date.**

State a **load ceiling** in hours per month, agree it honestly, and design around
it. If a task needs someone within a fixed window, restructure the task.

**Asynchronous by default.** No standing meetings, no response-time
expectations, no dependency on someone being available in a particular week.

**Personal circumstances are relevant to scoping, not to suitability.** Health,
other commitments and capacity shape what a role can be. They do not shape
whether the person is right for it.

---

## 6 · Compensation, agreed before work starts

Revenue share, deferred compensation, grant-funded stipend, equity, or in-kind.
Any of these is fine. **Unstated is not.**

Written before the first deliverable, in the MOU. The conversation is
straightforward before anyone has done anything and hard afterwards.

---

## 7 · The MOU — what it must contain

One template, replicated per person. **This is a legal document and needs a
lawyer.** Claude can draft the commercial substance and the scoping language; it
should not be the final instrument. Same counsel engagement as the international
transfer mechanism and the gendered-positioning advertising review.

1. **Role title**, and the **exact public description**, word for word,
   including how credentials appear on the site
2. **Scope of contribution** — what they deliver
3. **Explicit exclusions** — what they do not decide, sign off, or represent
4. **Credential representation** — what each party may claim about the other
5. **IP and ownership** — unambiguously, including work in progress
6. **Compensation** — form, amount, trigger
7. **Load and availability**, stated honestly
8. **Term, exit, and what happens to the work on exit**
9. **Confidentiality**, tied to the founder's-account handling rules
10. **Governing law** — Kenor International B.V., Sint Maarten

---

## 8 · The boundary document

One page, written **before** anyone starts. An hour's work. Not a contract — the
working agreement the MOU formalises.

Three lists, in plain language:

- **Andre decides:** …
- **[Name] delivers:** …
- **[Name] does not:** …

Plus the agreed public description, verbatim, and the load ceiling.

## 9 · Programme workflow

Six stages. Applies to any track carrying clinical, medical or population
sensitivity.

| Stage | Who | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Scoping | Founder | Track theme, ten protocol titles, state and population per protocol. One page |
| 2 · **Escalation sheet** | Founder drafts, **clinician signs** | Per theme: what means stop and see a doctor, what makes this protocol wrong for someone, what copy must never imply |
| 3 · Outline plus one | Founder | Full outline, plus one protocol written end to end at production depth |
| 4 · Substantiation | Contributor, async | Sourced research with DOIs, clinician candidates, population read |
| 5 · Clinical review | Clinician, paid | Outline and the one protocol read against the signed escalation sheet. Scope statement Kenor can cite |
| 6 · Build and ship | Founder | Remaining protocols against approved boundaries, lived-experience read, publication |

**Two departures worth understanding, because both save money:**

**The escalation sheet is signed before anything is written.** Safety boundaries
change what a protocol *is*. Discovered at significant build they cause rewrites,
not annotations — across ten protocols that is a fortnight against a quarter. It
is also the cheapest possible way to buy a clinician's signature: their first
paid task is reviewing ten pages, not fifteen thousand words.

**Handover is at outline-plus-one, never at significant build.** By the time
material feels substantial it is fixed — voice set, resources structured,
sequencing committed. A reviewer arriving then can only approve or cause
expensive damage, and most choose approve. That produces sign-off without
scrutiny, which is worse than no review, because it looks like the good outcome.

**Unavoidable cash: 3–6 hours of licensed clinical time per gated track.**

## 10 · Standing content rules for collaborators

These bind anyone producing material, not only staff.

- **No outcome promises.** Nothing predicts what will happen to a member.
- **No medical claims.** The line stays at regulation and never crosses into
  treating symptoms.
- **Nothing depicted as broken.** The state is not a defect. This applies with
  particular force to gendered and population-specific content, where the
  condition is routinely framed as a deficit.
- **Prohibited vocabulary:** quantum, frequency, manifest, rewire, spoken
  durations, a fifth step.
- **Credibility tiering.** Porges, HeartMath and Maté lead. Others are cited by
  mechanism, not by name, in clinical or investor contexts.
- **Sources or it does not ship.** Peer-reviewed, with dates and DOIs.

---

## 11 · Parity

⚠ **Where two tracks serve parallel populations, the standard must be
identical.** If one ships with a licensed reviewer and its counterpart ships with
a contributor and a workbook, that asymmetry will be noticed — by a journalist
before an investor.

Both wait, or neither ships.

---

## 12 · Applying this to someone new

1. Ask for credentials in writing, in their own words
2. Verify against the issuing register's own site
3. Draft the boundary document — three lists, one page
4. Agree the public description word for word
5. Agree compensation in writing
6. Execute the MOU
7. Then, and only then, begin work
