# Six months to a credible raise

**Traction thresholds, and the rollout that reaches them.**
14 September 2026.

---

## 1 · The principle

At your scale, **ratios matter more than volume.** An investor knows you had €15,000 and no
ad budget. They are not testing whether you can buy users. They are testing whether the
unit economics work when you do.

**250 paying members at 4% monthly churn is a far better story than 800 at 12%.** The first
says the product holds people. The second says you found a leaky bucket.

Set the gates on quality first, volume second.

---

## 2 · The gates — what makes month six credible

### Tier 1 · Must hit. Below these, do not raise on traction

| Metric | Threshold | Why this number |
|---|---|---|
| **Free → paid conversion** | **≥ 3.5%** | Below 3% and the funnel is the problem, not the reach. 3.5% is defensible freemium |
| **Monthly churn, paid** | **≤ 6%** | Above 6% annualises past 50% and every forecast built on it collapses |
| **Second-protocol open rate** | **≥ 45%** | The single best proxy for whether the content lands. One protocol is curiosity; two is use |
| **Paying members** | **≥ 180** | The floor at which conversion and churn are statistically meaningful rather than anecdotal |

⚠ **If conversion comes in at 2%, that is information, not failure — but it changes the
raise.** Path B assumes 4.2%. At 2% you raise Path A and fix the funnel first.

### Tier 2 · Strong. These make the conversation easy

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Paying members | **300+** |
| MRR | **€6,500+** |
| Cumulative free accounts | **4,000+** |
| Share on the €29 tier | **≥ 20%** — proves the upper tier is real, not theoretical |
| 30-day free retention | **≥ 30%** |
| Two written pilot findings reports | **Done** |
| One named reference customer | **Done** |

### Tier 3 · Qualitative, and worth more than it looks

- **Which protocols get opened most.** This decides which sector bundles you build first,
  and it is free data you only get by shipping
- **Twenty unsolicited pieces of written feedback.** A backer reads three of these and stops
  asking whether people want it
- **One podcast appearance that produced measurable signups.** Proves a repeatable channel
  exists

## 3 · What each gate actually tests

| Gate | What a backer learns |
|---|---|
| Conversion | Whether free-to-paid works at all. **The single most leveraged number in every forecast** |
| Churn | Whether the depth argument is true. Twelve resources per protocol is a retention claim — this is where it gets tested |
| Second-protocol open | Whether the method transfers between states, which is the whole architecture |
| €29 share | Whether the eight-track library justifies its own price |
| Pilot reports | Whether the corporate motion is real or theoretical |

## 5 · Instrument before you launch, not after

⚠ **Three months passes fast and impressions are not evidence.** These must be capturable
from week one:

| | |
|---|---|
| Signup source | Which channel produced each account |
| First protocol opened | And which track |
| **Second protocol opened — and when** | The most important event on the list |
| Free → paid, with days-to-convert | |
| Tier chosen at checkout | €19 or €29 |
| Cancellation, with month of tenure | |
| Workshop attendance → signup → conversion | Does the workshop actually work |

**None of this requires new infrastructure.** It requires deciding now what gets logged,
because retrofitting it in month four loses the first cohort entirely.

⚠ **This is measurement, not member-facing progress tracking.** Nothing here goes on a
screen a member sees. The prohibition on scores and streaks stands.

## 6 · The rollout risks, ranked

**1 · Sixty protocols in ninety days.** Three tracks took 2,316 hours. This is production
capacity, not ambition, and the audio pipeline's unresolved tone-break sits directly on the
critical path. **Mitigation: solve the audio problem in week one, and be willing to ship
six tracks rather than miss the pricing gate entirely.**

**2 · The clinical gate arrives mid-build.** Nutrition and Intimacy both need review.
**Mitigation: engage the clinician in week one, sequence those two tracks last.**

**3 · Paddle approval is outside your control.** Free tier launches regardless; the
conversion number does not. **Mitigation: apply week one, and if it slips, the month-three
decision point moves rather than the whole plan.**

**4 · Doing this alongside a full-time role.** The binding constraint is your hours, not the
money. **Mitigation: the free workshop is the highest-leverage two hours a month you
have — protect it before anything else.**

## 7 · What you can say in month six

If the Tier 1 gates hold:

> Eight tracks live. **[N] paying members at [X]% conversion and [Y]% monthly churn.**
> [Z]% chose the higher tier once the library was complete. Two organisational pilots
> delivered, both written up, one named reference. Built on $15,000 and no paid
> acquisition.
>
> We are not raising to find out whether people pay. We are raising to reach the people who
> already would.

**That is a materially different conversation from the one available today** — and the
difference is six months and fifteen thousand dollars, not a year and a bridge round.
