# SafeRise — Sex & Intimacy
## Working Prospectus · Provisioning Edition

**8 September 2026. Rewritten in house format from the source prospectus.**

The ten protocols, the coverage standard and the overlap-control logic are from
Andre's working prospectus and are adopted unchanged. What this rewrite adds is
reconciliation with the live platform: the resources that exist, the ones that
must be added back, and the ones whose renaming is a thirty-protocol decision
rather than a track-local one.

---

## 01 · Executive direction

**Track thesis.** Not a better-sex programme. A SafeRise track for adults
navigating sexuality as a domain of self-knowledge, regulation, courage,
relationship, embodiment and authorship.

It supports people who are exploring, recovering, changing, disclosing,
negotiating, questioning or reconnecting. It must be broad enough to include
conventional and unconventional sexual lives without treating either as the
default, and structured enough to distinguish curiosity, fantasy, identity,
action, consent, relationship agreement, health reality and embodied access.

> **Central track principle**
> SafeRise does not decide what you should want. It helps you become clear
> enough to decide what you will do with what you discover.

**Developmental arc.** Notice without judgment → understand without rushing →
communicate without hiding → negotiate without coercion → reconnect where
access has been interrupted → choose without surrendering authorship.

**Two simultaneous commitments.** Freedom from unnecessary shame, and
responsibility for consent, health, agreements, consequences and the autonomy
of others. Neither appears without the other.

**Hard boundary.** Psychoeducational and regulation-based. Not sex instruction,
not technique, not therapy. No protocol describes or depicts a sexual act.
Every protocol works the state around intimacy, never the act itself.

---

## 02 · Coverage standard

Each protocol has a distinct centre of gravity. Anything needed across most
protocols — consent, difficult conversations, journaling, values-based
decisions — lives in the resource architecture rather than consuming a slot.

Covered: sexual selfhood, identity, desire, fantasy, body confidence, being
seen. Exploration including kink, BDSM, toys, clubs and other consensual
experimentation. Relationship structure across monogamy, ethical non-monogamy
and negotiated alternatives. History, inexperience, comparison and regret.
Private sexuality and solo erotic life. Sexual seasons, shifting libido,
celibacy, ageing and life transitions. Mismatch in relationships that are
otherwise healthy. Shame, forbidden desire and self-forgiveness. Shutdown,
where desire remains but embodied access is interrupted. And living with an
STI, including disclosure, self-worth, dating and sexual future.

## 04 · Distinction between the closest protocols

| Protocol | Core question | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Sexual Self | Who am I sexually, and can I inhabit that person? | Identity, desire, confidence, fantasy-as-information, embodiment. |
| Sexual Exploration | What might I want to experience, and how do I explore it consciously? | Experimentation, kink, novel expression. |
| Forbidden Desire & Regret | What am I struggling to accept about what I want or have done? | Shame, moral conflict, regret, secrecy, self-forgiveness. |
| Sexual Shutdown | I want access, but my body withdraws. | Numbness, freeze, disconnection, inhibition, reconnection. |
| Desire Misalignment | We are connected, but our sexual wants differ. | Relational mismatch, frequency, style, initiation, negotiation. |

**Key distinctions.** Fantasy is not identity. Identity is not behaviour.
Desire is not decision. Curiosity is not consent. Consent does not override an
existing relationship agreement. A medical symptom does not automatically have
a psychological cause.

### Against Track 02

`t2-05 Intimacy Barrier` asks what happened *between us*. Protocol 09 asks what
happened *inside my sexual system*, such that desire may remain while access to
the body, arousal or expression no longer feels reliably available. Track 02 is
relational; this track works the member's own state and applies whether or not
a partner exists. The two do not significantly collide.

## 05 · Resource architecture

The track inherits the platform's resource system. It does not build a parallel
library.

### Two corrections to the source prospectus

**Safe Practice is missing and it is the resource this track needs most.** The
source prospectus §13 sets out referral thresholds, "no cause claims", "medical
issues stay medical" and adults-only provisioning — which is precisely what
Safe Practice carries — but the resource is absent from its architecture. It
belongs on all ten protocols and does more work on 09 and 10 than anywhere else
on the platform.

**The proposed renames are platform-wide.** Journal for Your Record, and Cue
Card – 4-Line Reset for Cue Card, affect 30 and 31 protocols respectively.
Legitimate, but they should be decided as a platform change rather than arrive
as a side effect of this track. The same applies to treating The Chosen Self as
a resource — it is currently a dashboard record item at `/record/chosen-self`.

The source prospectus is independently correct that **How This Works is the home
of the former Source Insights function**. That matches what the content store
already does and confirms retiring the separate slot.

### New conditional resources

Both adopted from the source prospectus. They are **track-conditional**, in the
same way Raising It is Track 03 only. The nine universal resources stay at nine.

**Desire Check.** Identifies what is actually present before deciding what it
means. Distinguishes: something I enjoy imagining; something I am curious to
learn about; something I might try once; something I want as part of my sexual
life; something I feel pressure to want; something I once wanted and no longer
do; something I do not yet understand; and uncertainty itself.

*Boundary.* It does not say whether to act — that is The Decision. It does not
negotiate with another person — that is Disclosure & Support and Consent &
Boundaries. Its job stops at clarity.

**Consent & Boundaries.** Affirmative and ongoing willingness, personal and
relational boundaries, negotiated limits, existing agreements, the right to
change your mind, stopping midway, pressure versus genuine willingness, power
differences and dependency, capacity, privacy, aftercare, and revisiting
agreements afterwards.

*Boundary.* Consent & Boundaries asks whether this can happen responsibly and
within everyone's limits. The Decision asks whether, knowing what you now know,
this is a choice you want to stand behind.

## 06 · Flagship territory · Sexual Shutdown & Reconnection

Distinct from Track 02's intimacy protocol. That one asks what happened between
us. This asks what happened inside my sexual system, such that desire may
remain while access to the body, arousal, spontaneity or expression no longer
feels reliably available.

May include: wanting connection intellectually while the body withdraws; losing
sensation when intimacy becomes real; freeze, numbness or absence; fantasy
remaining available while embodied sexuality feels inhibited; difficulty
receiving pleasure; a sexual self that shifts under expectation or perceived
pressure; suppression after an overwhelming experience; wanting closeness while
the body responds as though closeness is too much.

A distinctive reflection lens — the shifting sexual self. Who am I sexually
when nobody expects anything? Who do I become when someone desires me? Who do I
become when I am afraid of disappointing someone? Who am I in fantasy that I do
not permit myself to become in embodied life?

**Endpoint.** Not more sexuality. Less unnecessary obstruction between
awareness, desire, embodied readiness, choice and expression.

## 07 · Living With an STI/STD

A full protocol, not a health resource. The track treats it as a legitimate
human-development and intimacy experience while keeping diagnosis, testing,
treatment and risk guidance with qualified healthcare sources.

Addresses shock, anger, grief and shame after diagnosis; fear of being
undesirable; disclosure anxiety and timing; dating and rejection fear; trust
and relationship impact; responsibility without self-condemnation; confidence
and relaxation after diagnosis; a changing self-image; living with a condition
without it becoming the whole of a sexual identity; and reclaiming the
possibility of a sexual future.

**Clinical boundary.** SafeRise can support the emotional, relational and
authorship dimensions. It must not diagnose, estimate transmission risk for an
individual, prescribe treatment, or substitute for current medical guidance.

## 08 · Safety, scope and provisioning rules

- **Adults only.** Provisioned for adult sexuality and adult relationship
  contexts.
- **No diagnosis.** Never infer a medical, psychiatric or trauma diagnosis from
  a sexual experience.
- **No cause claims.** Shutdown, erectile difficulty, libido change and pain
  may have multiple contributors.
- **No pressure to explore.** Curiosity is not a mandate to act.
- **No pressure to disclose** beyond safety and legal responsibility.
  Disclosure resources prepare; they do not compel.
- **Consent is cross-cutting** — a reusable resource in every protocol, not a
  one-time lesson.
- **Relationship structures are not ranked.** Monogamy, ENM, polyamory,
  swinging and celibacy are consensual adult arrangements and SafeRise does not
  adjudicate between them.
- **Medical issues stay medical.** Persistent erectile difficulty, pain, STI
  treatment, medication effects and unexplained libido change need health
  evaluation.
- **Specialist support is normalised** — sex therapy, couples therapy,
  trauma-informed therapy, pelvic-floor care, medical consultation.
- **No hidden defaults.** Editorial review must catch heteronormative and
  monogamy-as-default assumptions before publication.

## 09 · Clinical advisory partner

This track is a co-development lane, not a retrospective review. The clinical
partner should: review trauma-sensitive language and pacing; help distinguish
consensual exploration from coercion or abuse; review the consent and boundary
resources; advise on shutdown, shame and identity-sensitive protocols; support
referral thresholds; review the boundary where sexual behaviour intersects with
compulsivity; contribute specialist perspective to How This Works; help prevent
shame-based or default-assuming framing; and support medically responsible
language on sexual function, STIs and libido change.

**This makes the clinical-collaborator decision a dependency of this track**,
independent of any decision about site presence.

## 10 · Content architecture

Four steps, unchanged: Recognition → Regulation → Release → Rise. The track
changes the subject matter, not the method.

Recognition identifies the state, desire, conflict, body response or relational
reality without turning it into a verdict. Regulation creates enough steadiness
to stay with the experience rather than force an answer. Release loosens the
secondary struggle — shame, self-argument, pressure, performance, catastrophic
meaning, inherited expectation. Rise restores perspective and authorship: what
can be chosen, communicated, paused, explored or left alone.

Where deeper inquiry is appropriate, the layered audit runs event or desire →
immediate meaning → pattern familiarity → deeper human concern → protective
intelligence → compassion → authorship. **Do not tell the member what their
deeper wound is.** Invite the inquiry and leave interpretation with them.

## 11 · Evidence plan

Before publication: qualified sex-therapy and sexual-health expertise for
clinical and educational review; relationship-science sources for desire
discrepancy, attachment, communication and consensual non-monogamy; current
public-health sources for STI information and referral language; appropriate
medical references for sexual-function topics; consent and safeguarding
guidance for adult exploration; trauma-sensitive review for shutdown, regret
and identity-sensitive content; and inclusivity review across orientations,
identities and consensual adult relationship structures.

How This Works carries these authorities in plain language. The guided protocol
never reads like a textbook.
