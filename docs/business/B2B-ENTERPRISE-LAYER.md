## 1 · The measurement problem, and its resolution

This is the crux of every HR conversation and it needs a settled answer before
the first call.

**HR must report something to renew a budget line. Track 03 forbids outcome
claims, and the platform promises usage is never reported by name.**

| Layer | Reportable | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Provisioning | ✅ | "400 people have access." The employer already knows |
| Live session attendance | ✅ | Attendance at a scheduled event, not private use |
| Voluntary testimonial | ✅ | The employee chose to say it |
| Employer-run anonymous survey | ✅ | Their instrument, their data, never touches SafeRise |
| Private protocol usage | ❌ | The promise. Breaking it destroys the reason it works |
| Individual records or journals | ❌ | Device-only. Technically unavailable, not merely withheld |

### The resolution

**Bundle a countable live component into every licence.** Live delivery is
countable; private use is not. A quarterly facilitated session gives HR
attendance figures, session feedback and a visible programme to point at — while
the private layer stays dark.

> "You will be able to report on the sessions. You will never be able to report
> on the individuals — and that is exactly why people use it."

⚠ **Open decision.** Anonymised aggregate activation — "62% of the population has
activated an account" — identifies nobody, is standard EAP practice, and makes
renewal materially easier. It also reopens a door the architecture was built to
close. **The answer must be instant and identical every time a buyer asks.**
Decide it before the first enterprise call, not during one.

## 2 · Seats, and why the privacy argument survives the mechanism

Bulk seat entitlement is a structural build item — issue and revoke seats,
individual journals invisible to the employer. That is the right mechanism. The
point that matters sits one level up:

**An employee who has to *request* a seat has disclosed something.** The request
is itself a signal. The entire proposition rests on nobody having to declare a
difficulty to get help.

**So: provision the whole population by default, revoke on exit.** Seats as an
entitlement ledger, not an application process. This costs nothing to build
differently and preserves the only thing that makes the product work inside an
employer.

It also removes seat-management overhead, renewal true-ups, and the conversation
about who qualifies.

## 3 · Licence the whole library, not the work track

The instinct is to licence Track 03. Don't.

An employee struggling in a performance review is very often the same employee
struggling at home. Restricting a corporate licence to the work track sells a
third of the person and puts SafeRise in the same narrow box every workplace
wellbeing tool already occupies.

Marginal delivery cost of including the other tracks is **zero** — the content is
written and hosted. It roughly doubles perceived value at no cost and makes the
offer hard to compare against anything in the category.

**Sell full library access. Position Track 03 as the reason they buy; the rest is
the reason employees actually use it.**

## 4 · The procurement advantage

**Device-only records collapse the security review.** This is worth more in a B2B
sale than any feature, and it is not written down anywhere else.

When a wellbeing vendor is onboarded, procurement and InfoSec run a review
proportional to the sensitivity of what the vendor holds. Most hold detailed
emotional and behavioural data on employees. SafeRise holds almost none.

| Data | Where it sits |
|---|---|
| Journals, records, protocol progress | **The employee's own device.** Never transmitted |
| Account and authentication | Supabase, Frankfurt (eu-central-1) |
| Billing | Employer-level, no employee data |

**Consequences for the sale**

- The DPA is short, because the processing is minimal
- Article 9 special-category risk is largely absent from the employer's side —
  there is no health data held by the vendor to breach
- A security questionnaire has few uncomfortable answers
- EU data residency pre-answers the standard European objection
- No employee-level data means no employer liability for what the vendor does
  with it

**Lead the technical review with this.** Say on the first call that their InfoSec
review will be unusually quick, and why. It converts the longest, most painful
stage of enterprise procurement into a short one.

⚠ The Chapter V question remains open. Remote operational access from Sint
Maarten to Frankfurt data may itself constitute a transfer. A European
enterprise's DPO will find it. See `docs/ARTICLE-30-RECORD.md`.

## 5 · The selling problem Track 03 creates

**Track 03's own rules forbid the claim the buyer wants to hear.**

Prohibited on that track beyond the platform list: *productivity · optimise ·
performance target · output · efficiency · high performer · earning potential ·
best results · maximise.* Plus no implied outcomes — nobody will be promoted,
paid more, or assessed more favourably.

Those are precisely the words an HR or L&D buyer uses to justify a budget line
upward. The track refuses all of them on purpose.

**Do not solve this by breaking the rule.** Sell four things that are true:

| Sell | Why it works without an outcome claim |
|---|---|
| **The facilitated day** | A defined deliverable. They buy a day, not a result |
| **Privacy by architecture** | Structural, not a promise. See §4 |
| **Verifiable qualification** | Three completed certifications. Competence is checkable; outcomes are not claimed |
| **The refusal itself** | A wellbeing product that declines to add a second scoreboard. Leadership recognises the cynicism it answers |

**The line that opens the conversation:** *"Everything you have bought before
required someone to raise their hand. This doesn't."*

### MEDDICC against a no-outcome-claims product

The qualification method leads with quantified metrics. That is a direct
collision. **Resolution: the metric sizes the current state, never the promised
delta.**

| | Applied here |
|---|---|
| **Metrics** | The buyer's own numbers on the *problem* — turnover cost, rehire cost, absence, cost of a departed senior hire. Never a claimed improvement |
| **Economic buyer** | CHRO, People Director, or COO |
| **Decision criteria** | Privacy architecture, facilitator qualification, format fit. Compete here, not on outcomes |
| **Decision process** | Procurement, legal, DPA. Frankfurt residency answers the GDPR question |
| **Identify pain** | Track 03's own material — assessment, conflict that cannot be exited, burnout, decision fatigue |
| **Champion** | Usually an HR lead who watched previous wellbeing spend fail on trust |
| **Competition** | EAPs and meditation apps. Both lose on trust; neither can say records are never opened to leadership |

## 7 · Two markets, two price books

Regional employer budgets are structurally different, not a discount case. A
200-person resort in Sint Maarten and a 200-person SaaS company in Amsterdam are
the same headcount and entirely different buyers.

| | Caribbean | Europe |
|---|---|---|
| Currency | USD | EUR |
| Wellbeing as a budget line | Rarely exists | Established, often mandated |
| Procurement | Relationship-led, fast | Process-led, slow |
| Decision maker | Owner, GM, HR Manager | CHRO, People Director, committee |
| Typical cycle | 2–8 weeks | 3–9 months |
| Payer | **Often not the employer** | The employer |

⚠ **Amounts are not carried forward** — the flat bands that sat here are retired.
Whatever replaces them, the structural rule holds: **band by headcount, never by
usage.** Banding on company size never touches who used what, so the privacy
promise survives the pricing model intact. Regional pricing is never published on
the public site, and the two books never appear together.

### The payer is often not the employer

Qualify for **funding route**, not just budget. Ask early: *"Would this come out
of your budget, or is there a training fund or insurer programme it could sit
under?"*

| Source | Applies to |
|---|---|
| Development finance — CDB, IDB | Workforce development, health, resilience |
| Health insurers | Provision that reduces claims. Insurer-funded, employer-delivered |
| Government workforce funds | Training levies, skills development |
| **Sector associations** | Hospitality and trade bodies buying once for many members |
| Grant programmes | NGO and public-health funded, often multi-year |
| Parent company | Regional subsidiaries of international groups — **budget sits abroad, European pricing may apply** |

⚠ **The association route is the highest-leverage regional play.** One hospitality
association buying member-wide reaches dozens of employers through a single sale
and a single relationship. Worth pursuing ahead of individual employers.

**Build a parent-company check into qualification** so subsidiaries are priced on
the right book.

## 8 · Target profiles, ranked

| # | Profile | Market | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **BPO / contact centres** | Caribbean | Attrition measured monthly, costed per head, already funded. Tens of thousands of seats in Jamaica and the DR. English-operating |
| 2 | **Remote-first companies** | Europe | They physically cannot provide office-based wellbeing. A device-based, remote-delivered, privacy-preserving platform is the only format that works. Largely unserved |
| 3 | **Tech / SaaS scale-ups** | Europe | The warm network. Burnout discussed openly. Engineer retention is expensive and visible |
| 4 | **Hospitality associations** | Caribbean | One sale reaches many employers. Local in-person delivery is the margin advantage — a half-day on the island costs a taxi |
| 5 | **Financial services, trust, legal** | Both | Review culture, visible assessment. Closest Track 03 match, best budgets. **Check for a parent company** |
| 6 | **Healthcare** | Both | Burnout named and unstigmatised. Retention existential. Often grant or insurer funded |
| 7 | **Energy — Guyana / Trinidad** | Caribbean | Fastest-growing corporate market in the region. Duty-of-care arrives with international operators |
| 8 | Government, utilities, ports, airports | Caribbean | Real budgets, slow cycles, tender requirements |
| — | **Law enforcement · fire · military · cruise crew** | Both | ⚠ **Held.** Highest resonance, highest trauma exposure, empty clinical layer |

**Run one regional and one European profile in parallel.** Regional closes in
weeks, European in months — so the pipeline does not go quiet while enterprise
deals mature.

## 10 · The launch kit decides renewal

Activation is where corporate wellbeing dies. The employer announces it once and
nothing happens.

**Supply the whole internal launch** — announcement email from leadership,
intranet copy, a one-page explanation of what it is and is not, a three-week
follow-up sequence, and the first session scheduled *before* the announcement so
there is somewhere to go.

**The privacy statement is the activation lever.** Employees do not open
employer-provided wellbeing tools because they assume someone is watching. Say
plainly, in the employer's own announcement, that records never leave the device
and the employer cannot see usage. That paragraph does more for activation than
any feature.

## 12 · What a proposal must never say

- No productivity, efficiency, output, optimise, performance target
- No retention, absenteeism or turnover claims — outcome promises in procurement clothing
- **No aggregate reporting offer** unless §1 is ruled the other way
- No clinical claim. Not treatment; the escalation gates exist because it isn't
- No diagnosis of the organisation's culture from outside

**What replaces them:** a defined deliverable, a named facilitator with verifiable
certifications, an agreed format, a privacy architecture that can be described
mechanically, and an honest statement of what the day will not do.

The live page's *Not the right fit if* block is the strongest trust device on the
site. Put its equivalent in every proposal.
