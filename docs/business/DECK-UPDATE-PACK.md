## 1 · The correction that matters most

### Slide 13 · Revenue architecture — the subscription line is superseded

**Currently reads:**

> Subscription · B2C individual · **€19 → €29 → €39** · Cumulative. Adding a track never
> removes one.

**That ladder was superseded and fixed at source in SR-381**, with a dated supersession
comment in `content/tracks.js`. The live model is:

> **Subscription · B2C individual · Track 01 free with an account · €19 a month or €190 a
> year for every other track, including every track added after them.**
> Adding a track never costs more.

**Why it changed, in one line for the deck:** a cumulative ladder means every new track is
a new pricing decision and an upgrade prompt to existing members. One price means a new
track simply arrives — which is the stronger offer and the one that scales to eight tracks.

⚠ **This appears in more than one place.** Check slide 13, any pricing mention on slide 4
or 12, and the GTM deck's consumer engine slide, which shows **1:1 at €79 / €199** where
the settled figures are **€129 / €349 for three**. The two decks currently disagree with
each other.

## 3 · New material to add

### 3a · The three-layer model — new slide, after slide 4

This is the strongest structural argument produced since the deck was written, and it is
what makes the nutrition and fitness tracks defensible rather than opportunistic.

> **Three layers. Most programmes work one.**
>
> **Capacity** — noticing the state, regulating it, recovering choice. *What SafeRise
> trains.*
> **Substrate** — sleep, nutrition, training load, chronic stress. *Whether that capacity
> is physically available.*
> **Application** — conversations, decisions, performance. *Where the recovered choice
> lands.*
>
> The arrow runs both ways. Degraded capacity produces the decisions that wreck sleep;
> poor sleep degrades capacity further. SafeRise is the point at which a person can
> interrupt that loop rather than be carried by it.

⚠ **Do not claim attention governance sits above physiology.** Sleep loss measurably
degrades prefrontal–amygdala connectivity and no technique restores that. The narrow claim
is defensible; the broad one is not, and a clinical reviewer will catch it.

**Do not use the acute glucose–willpower model.** Ego depletion has largely failed
replication.

### 3b · Sector bundles — add to the corporate section

> **The same method. A different curriculum.** Healthcare and emergency · Finance, legal
> and professional services · Technology and engineering · Manufacturing and logistics ·
> Sales and client-facing · Leadership and people managers.
>
> Each gets a different route through the same library, with facilitated sessions
> following that route. Every seat still carries the whole library, so nobody has to
> predict who needs what.

### 3c · Core library, always included

Eight tracks available to every member and every organisational seat: Personal
Transformation · Professional Performance · Executive Presence · Relationship Healing ·
Embodied Nutrition · Strength & Return · **Sleep & Recovery** · Elevation Series.

⚠ **Five of those have not shipped.** Mark them *in development* wherever they are listed,
or the deck promises what cannot be delivered.

### 3d · Sleep & Recovery — the identified gap

Worth one line in the expansion section. It has the strongest mechanistic evidence of any
substrate input, is the least stigmatised entry point for a corporate buyer, is named in
three of the six sector bundles, and **does not exist.** It ships without waiting on a
clinician, which is why it should be built first.

## 4 · Slides to amend

### Slide 17 · Team

The deck lists **Kirsten Morris — research and grant strategy, certified in Functional
Family Therapy.** Elsewhere she is described as standing in on the clinical side. **The
risk register on slide 20 correctly says no licensed clinician is contracted.**

**Make the two consistent.** If Kirsten is not the contracted clinician, do not let a
reader infer that she is — a backer who assumes the clinical gate is closed and later
learns it is not will discount everything else.

### Slide 20 · Risk register — add two

**Facilitator capacity is the ceiling on B2B, not demand.** Roughly four hours a month of
delivery capacity exists today. A Foundation account consumes about six hours a year, a
Practice account about twenty-two, a Partner account about sixty. **Partner cannot be sold
before a second facilitator exists.** Selling a tier the business cannot deliver is worse
than not selling it.

**The organisation data model does not exist.** Seats currently belong to individuals;
nothing in the platform represents an organisation, provisions seats, or produces the
quarterly seats-in-use figure that is promised in writing.

### Slide 15 · Defensibility

The trademark note — an adjacent iOS product marketing itself as *"Regulate. Rewire.
Rise."* — is correctly flagged. **Move it from a footnote to the risk register.** A name
collision discovered after launch is expensive; discovered before, it is a decision.

### Slide 19 · Forecast

The claim that year one lands within €1,100 across every scenario is strong and true for
the reason given — the delivery lines are calendar-capped. **Say the cap out loud on the
slide:** it is roughly four hours a month of founder delivery capacity. That converts an
apparent coincidence into a credible constraint.

## 6 · Two things to decide before the next backer conversation

**Founder salary.** The financial model sets it to zero. That is honest today and it makes
the raise look cheaper than a funded version would be. A backer will ask what you intend
to pay yourself.

**Which corporate pricing is real.** The pitch deck says annual access is **banded by
headcount, never by usage** — and that is the right answer, because per-seat pricing
implies counting individual usage and contradicts the device-only privacy commitment that
is load-bearing everywhere else in the deck.

⚠ **Any per-seat figure anywhere must go.** Banded by headcount is the position that
survives scrutiny.
