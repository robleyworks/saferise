## MEDDICC against a no-outcome-claims product

The qualification method leads with **M — quantified metrics.** Track 03 forbids
outcome claims outright. That is a direct collision and it needs a settled answer
before the first discovery call.

**Resolution: the metric sizes the current state, never the promised delta.**

| MEDDICC | Applied here |
|---|---|
| **Metrics** | The buyer's own numbers on the *problem* — turnover cost, rehire cost, absence, cost of a departed senior hire. Never a claimed improvement |
| **Economic buyer** | CHRO, People Director, or COO. Familiar territory |
| **Decision criteria** | Privacy architecture, facilitator qualification, format fit. Compete on these, not on outcomes |
| **Decision process** | Procurement, legal, DPA. Frankfurt processing is already the standing preference and answers the GDPR question |
| **Identify pain** | Track 03's own material — being assessed, conflict that cannot be exited, burnout, decision fatigue |
| **Champion** | Usually an HR lead who has watched previous wellbeing spend fail on trust |
| **Competition** | EAPs and meditation apps. Both lose on trust; neither can say records are never opened to leadership |

**The champion's real problem is the one to solve.** Most HR leaders have bought
wellbeing before and watched engagement collapse because staff assumed the
employer could see the data. Device-only records are a structural answer to the
objection that killed their last purchase. That is the discovery conversation.

## The central selling problem

**Track 03's own rules forbid the claim the buyer wants to hear.**

Prohibited on this track, beyond the platform list: *productivity · optimise ·
performance target · output · efficiency · high performer · earning potential ·
best results · maximise.* Plus: no implied outcomes, no suggestion anyone will be
promoted, paid more, or assessed more favourably.

Those are precisely the words an HR or L&D buyer uses to justify a budget line
upward. The track refuses all of them, on purpose.

**Do not solve this by breaking the rule.** Solve it by selling something else
that is true.

### What to sell instead

| Sell | Why it works without an outcome claim |
|---|---|
| **The facilitated event** | A half-day is a defined deliverable. They are buying a day, not a result. No claim required |
| **Privacy by architecture** | Corporate wellness fails because staff assume HR sees the data. SafeRise's records are device-only and never opened to leadership. That is structural, not a promise |
| **The refusal itself** | *You are being graded. This platform is not the one doing it.* A wellness product that declines to add a second scoreboard is a genuinely differentiated position, and leadership recognises the cynicism it's answering |
| **Continuity after the event** | Most corporate wellness ends when the facilitator leaves. Annual access is the answer to "and then what" |
| **Escalation discipline** | Where a situation is targeted — undermining, discrimination, unsafe environment — the protocol stops and routes to people with authority. That protects the employer as much as the employee |
| **Verifiable qualification** | Certified Jungian Practitioner · Certified Somatic Coach · Meditation Coach. Three completed certifications, all checkable. Qualification is not an outcome claim — it is the one credibility lever the track's rules leave fully open |
| **Lived proximity to the subject** | A career in strategic C-suite and consultative selling means the founder has occupied the exact rooms Track 03 describes. Not a claim about results — a claim about knowing the territory, and it survives any scrutiny |

**The line that carries a room:** *Nobody has to disclose anything to anyone.*
It is already on the page and it is the strongest sentence there.

## The first-reference problem

You have zero corporate references. This is the entire difficulty, and everything
above is secondary to it.

**The first contract is worth more as a reference than as revenue.**

| Move | Terms |
|---|---|
| Offer the first organisation a **free half-day** | In exchange for: a written quotable reference, permission to name them, and 20 minutes of feedback afterwards |
| Second and third | Founding rate €1,800 regardless of size, same reference terms |
| Fourth onward | Standard pricing |

Giving away €1,800 to get a named reference from a recognised local employer is a
straightforwardly good trade. Without one, every conversation starts from zero.
With one, the second conversation starts from *they did this at [X]*.

**⚠ Do not give away annual access.** Give the event, sell the continuity. If
access is free the recurring line never establishes a price.

## What a proposal must not say

Worth writing on the inside of the folder.

- No productivity, efficiency, output, optimise, performance target
- No retention, absenteeism or turnover claims — these are outcome promises in
  procurement clothing
- No aggregate reporting offer. *No usage reporting by name, ever* is already
  published; offering anonymised aggregates re-opens the door the architecture closes
- No clinical claim. The platform is not treatment, and the escalation gates
  exist precisely because it isn't
- No diagnosis of the organisation's culture from the outside

**What replaces them:** a defined deliverable, a named facilitator with three
verifiable certifications, an agreed format, a privacy architecture that can be
described mechanically, and an honest statement of what the day will not do.

**Lead the proposal with the credential stack.** Certified Somatic Coach is the
one that most directly justifies facilitating bodily-state work in a workplace,
and it is the qualification most corporate wellbeing facilitators do not hold.
The track forbids claiming results; it does not forbid evidencing competence, and
that is the lever to use hard.

The page's *Not the right fit if* block is the strongest trust device on the
site. Put its equivalent in every proposal.
