## The structural consequence, before the prices

**A free Track 01 does not need the payment rail.**

It can go live while CX Pay / Orco underwriting is still in process. Launch stops
being gated on a bank and only monetisation is.

This splits Gate A in two:

| Gate | Requires payments | Blocks |
|---|---|---|
| **A-free** — Track 01 live, 20+ users, ≥50% completion, ≥30% 7-day return | No | Nothing. Can start immediately |
| **A-paid** — rail live, refund-tested, first transaction settled | Yes | Subscriptions, 1:1, workshops |

The retention evidence a supporter needs for T2 — completion and return — comes
entirely from A-free. **The single largest external dependency stops sitting on
the critical path.**

## The free tier

**Track 01 — Personal Transformation. Complete and free.**
All 10 protocols, full resource library, guided audio, and the record.

Three independent competitor signals in the market intel ledger pointed at this
before it was proposed: every operator in the segment leads with free or
near-free and monetises depth. SafeRise had no entry point at all. It also
neutralises the live objection — that a competing app sells lifetime access for
$27 — because free beats $27 and depth is what gets sold after.

### ⚠ The cost, stated honestly

Track 01 holds Anxiety Reset and Shame Dissolution. It is the **broadest-appeal
content in the platform.** The paid tracks are narrower and more situational —
relationship difficulty, professional performance — conditions people move into
over time rather than arrive with.

**Consequence:** conversion runs slower than standard freemium. The forecast
below uses 5% rather than the 10%+ a thinner free tier would produce. Some people
will find Track 01 fully sufficient and never convert.

**Worth taking anyway.** Distribution is the bottleneck, not content, and nothing
fixes distribution like genuinely good free work. But hold one lever in reserve.

**Fallback if conversion lands under 3%:** gate the guided audio. Free keeps the
written protocols, resources and record; Andre's recorded meditations become
paid. Do not deploy this pre-emptively — it costs the honesty that makes the
free tier work.

## The subscription

**One fee. Everything except Track 01, which is free anyway.**

| Tier | Monthly | Annual | Available | Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Founding** | €19 | €190 | First 100 members, **locked for life** | Launch |
| **Standard** | €29 | €290 | — | When 4 paid tracks live (~day 165) |
| **Full** | €39 | €390 | — | When 7 paid tracks live (~day 325) |

Annual is ten months — two free.

**Why €19 founding.** Track 01 was €19. It is now free, and €19 buys everything
else. That is a clean, honest, memorable story, and it uses a number already on
the tracker. 100 founding members at €19 is €1,900/month of permanent base.

**Narrate the founding rate.** The market intel ledger noted that the strongest
pricing page in the competitive set works as a founder confession — the reasoning
is exposed, which turns a discount into a reciprocal ask. Say plainly that the
price is low because there are two paid tracks today and seven coming, and that
founding members carry the risk of the ones not yet built. Narrating it costs
nothing.

**Elevation Series** stays outside the subscription: €222 one-time,
eligibility-gated, absent from public marketing. Unchanged.

## Workshops — all remote

Remote is a strategic upgrade, not a compromise. In-person capped the addressable
audience at an island of ~44,000. Remote opens it to the entire free-track base
wherever they are, removes the venue line from the budget, allows larger rooms,
and produces a recording that becomes a reusable asset.

| Format | Price | Job | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Intro — remote, 60 min** | Free | Top of funnel. Drives free-track signups, fills 1:1 slots | Monthly |
| **Personal — remote** | €59/person | Primary group revenue line | Monthly from M3 |
| **Relationship — remote** | €139/couple | Highest value per head | Quarterly from M4 |
| **Corporate lunch-and-learn — remote** | Free | B2B entry offer | On request |

**Attendance assumption:** Personal starts at 12 and grows to 20. Relationship
starts at 5 couples and grows to 8. Both conservative for remote delivery.

**Note on remote Relationship work.** Couples work is harder over video than in a
room — more so than individual work. Cap it lower, expect to do more active
facilitation, and consider a shorter format with a follow-up rather than one long
session.

**Record every paid workshop.** Replays become a member benefit, a sales asset,
and eventually the seed of the Elevation Series delivery layer.

## Premium 1:1

| Stage | Single | 3-session pack | Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Launch** | €79 | €199 (€66/session) | Day 45 |
| **+40% — waitlist exists** | €110 | €279 (€93/session) | ~M7 |
| **Second increase** | €155 | €395 | Waitlist + published testimonials |
| **Year 2 target** | €199–275 | — | Track record established |

**Capacity: 3 per week, hard cap.** 12 sessions/month.

### What €79 actually buys

The constraint on this line is the calendar, not demand. Three slots a week is
three slots a week at any price — so a lower price does not produce more
sessions, it produces **filled** sessions.

At €275 with no testimonials and no published track record, one slot a week is a
realistic fill rate. At €79 all three fill from launch and produce the case
studies that make €275 sayable later.

**€79 buys evidence, not revenue.** A sound trade for the early period, provided
it is treated as a stage with defined exit conditions rather than a resting
price.

### Why the 3-pack matters more than the discount

Three sessions is roughly where someone can feel a shift. That makes a pack far
more likely to produce a usable testimonial than three unconnected one-offs —
and testimonials are the asset that unlocks every subsequent price increase.

**Push the pack, not the single.** Present €199/3 as the default and the single
session as the exception.

### On the ladder

40% takes €79 to €110 — still below half the original figure and at the low end
of the market for somatic or coaching work. Plan on **two increases, not one.**
Write both trigger conditions down now so the second one isn't a decision made
under pressure later.

## What the forecast says

**Going remote more than paid for the 1:1 price cut.** Year 1 rises from €47,297
to €50,065 despite cutting the session rate 71% — because remote workshops run
monthly instead of every other month and hold more people. The two changes
together are net positive.

**Workshops become the second-largest line.** €13,526, ahead of 1:1 at €10,264.
That inverts the earlier picture, where 1:1 carried the first eight months. The
monthly remote workshop is now the thing to protect in the calendar.

**All four lines land within €5,000 of each other.** No single point of failure —
unusual and genuinely valuable at this stage. If corporate stalls or conversion
underperforms, the business does not fall over.

**Delivery load at steady state is ~18 hours/month:** 12 one-to-ones, one paid
workshop, one free intro, and a quarterly couples session amortised. Roughly four
hours a week of live delivery. Sustainable alongside build work — but it does not
leave room for a second weekly workshop, so resist adding one.

**Gate B clears around M3–M4**, ahead of the day-90 target.

**Against the raise.** Month 7 sits at ~€4,100/month with 58 paid members and
~1,000 free. Thin on subscription metrics alone. The corporate contracts and the
free-base growth rate carry that conversation, not MRR.

## Years 2–3

Modelled with cohort-tracked pricing (€19 founding / €29 / €39), churn applied
proportionally across cohorts, and trailing conversion drawn only from a **warm
pool** — the last six months of unconverted signups. People who have sat in the
free tier for half a year and not converted mostly never will; treating the whole
base as convertible produces a runaway curve and a fictional forecast.

### Three scenarios

| | Y1 | Y2 | Y3 | 3-year | M36 paid | M36 free | M36 sub MRR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Conservative** | €53,545 | €197,039 | €333,506 | €584,090 | 599 | 14,954 | €23,131 |
| **Base** | €53,903 | €240,656 | €502,681 | €797,240 | 1,129 | 22,012 | €43,641 |
| **Optimistic** | €54,627 | €278,021 | €662,499 | €995,147 | 1,644 | 28,654 | €63,636 |

*Scenarios vary two inputs only: free-signup growth and churn. Conservative runs
10%/10%/9% monthly churn; base 9%/8%/7%; optimistic 8%/7%/6%.*

*Y1 restated at €53.9K here versus €50,065 earlier — the earlier figure used a
single blended price; this model tracks the three price cohorts properly.*

### The finding that matters

**Year 1 is identical across all three scenarios — €53.5K, €53.9K, €54.6K.**

Year 1 revenue is set by **delivery capacity**, not growth. 1:1, workshops and
corporate are capped by the calendar and produce roughly the same total whatever
the funnel does. Subscription is too small in year 1 to move the number.

**Consequence:** year 1 is highly predictable and can be committed to. Years 2–3
diverge by 2× because they are subscription-led, and subscription depends
entirely on an input that does not yet exist.

### The honest gap

Every scenario turns on free signups running at 400–1,200 per month by year 3.

**There is currently no mechanism producing any of them.** The site is noindex.
There is no ad budget in the plan. There is no list, no audience, no SEO
position, no channel. The forecast assumes distribution that has not been built.

This is not a modelling flaw — it is the actual unsolved problem, and it is the
single thing a raise would change. Ad spend, or a partnership, or an SEO
position, is what converts the conservative curve into the optimistic one.

**Plan against the conservative case. Treat anything above it as upside.**

### The strategic arc

Delivery-capped revenue as a share of the total:

| Year | 1:1 + workshops | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Y1 | €23,790 | **44%** |
| Y2 | €39,104 | **16%** |
| Y3 | €51,512 | **10%** |

The business starts as a service business and becomes a product business. Both
transitions are visible in the numbers: 1:1 and workshops carry year 1 almost
entirely, then flatten against their calendar ceiling while subscription
compounds past them.

**This is the reason the free tier matters more than the pricing.** The capped
lines cannot grow. Only the base can.

### ⚠ The margin figure is misleading

90–96% margin is what appears when **the founder's time costs nothing.**

A solo operator delivering 12 sessions a month, a paid workshop, a free
workshop, support for a growing member base, and building eight tracks is not
free labour. Draw a market salary and year 1 is **negative**, year 2 comfortable,
year 3 genuinely profitable.

State it that way to anyone who reads this. A forecast showing 96% margin on a
service business invites exactly one question, and having answered it first is
worth more than the number.

**Hiring is not optional in the base case.** The VA from month 18 and a
facilitator in year 3 are in the cost model because without them the delivery
lines cap out and the member base outgrows one person's support capacity.

### Against the raise

| Point | Value |
|---|---|
| Month 7 | ~€4,200/month, 66 paid members, ~1,100 free |
| Month 12 | €5,091 sub MRR, 189 paid, 2,974 free |
| Month 24 | €23,798 sub MRR, 632 paid, 10,878 free |

Month 7 is thin on subscription metrics for a $150–300K pre-seed. What carries
that conversation is the corporate contracts, the free-base growth rate, and the
clinical co-sign — not MRR.

The stronger argument is structural: **year 1 revenue is capacity-bound and
provable; years 2–3 are distribution-bound.** The raise buys the one input the
model cannot generate on its own.
