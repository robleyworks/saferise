## 1 · Programme architecture — three tiers

The strongest idea in the source. Maps cleanly onto three different buyers with
three different budgets.

| Tier | Positioning | Buyer | Budget source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Preventative** | Baseline provision for the whole population | HR / Chief People Officer | Wellbeing |
| **Professional performance** | Capability under pressure, department-specific | Business unit leader — VP Sales, VP Engineering, Head of Trading | Enablement or departmental |
| **Acute recovery** | Support after an incident | C-suite, Chief Legal, Chief Risk | Operational or risk |

**Why this matters commercially:** it is three sales into one account, on three
budget lines, without the second and third depending on the first. It also
matches the department-track finding — the middle tier bypasses HR entirely.

already established.

                        ~30–55% new writing
    ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
        SHARED BASE   — Tracks 01–03, 30 protocols, 308 resources
                        Recognise · Regulate · Release · Rise

## 4 · State-based navigation — already built

The source proposes routing by current state rather than by course structure.
**SafeRise already does this.** The dashboard routes on Agitated / Unsteady /
Numb, and every protocol is tagged to one.

| Source proposal | SafeRise equivalent |
|---|---|
| Racing / anxious / panic | **Agitated** |
| Numb / exhausted / overwhelmed | **Numb** |
| Grounded / ready | **Unsteady** → or straight to practice |

independently.

## 5 · Content format blueprint

Useful, and partially new.

| Format | Length | Purpose | SafeRise status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acute tool** | 90 sec – 3 min | Use during or immediately after an incident | ⚠ **Gap.** Shortest current resources are longer |
| **Transition tool** | 5–10 min | End of shift, before going home | Partially covered |
| **Foundation** | ~5 min | Why the body does this | Covered by resource library |

**The acute tool is a genuine gap.** Every sector in Tier C needs something
usable in a patrol car, between cases, or during an incident. Nothing currently
in the library is sized for that.

○ Worth scoping a short-form format across existing protocols before building any
sector track.

## 7 · ⚠ Compliance filter — what must be rewritten

The source violates SafeRise language rules throughout. Every item below would
have to be rewritten before use.

### Prohibited outcome claims

| Source language | Status |
|---|---|
| "reducing Mean Time to Resolution" | **Prohibited** — outcome claim |
| "protecting your capital from emotional market execution" | **Prohibited** |
| "revenue protector" · "maximising performance ROI" | **Prohibited** |
| "decreased absenteeism" · "higher retention scores" | **Prohibited** |
| "reduced medical errors" · "lower nursing turnover" | **Prohibited** |
| "reduced tactical errors" · "lower PTSD claims" | **Prohibited**, and clinically reckless |
| "peak performance visualisation" | **Prohibited** — "peak performance" is on the Track 03 list |
| "extended clinical career longevity" | **Prohibited** |

### Prohibited mechanism language

| Source language | Status |
|---|---|
| **"discharge" residual survival energy** | **Explicitly retired.** On the rejected-language list |
| "shaking to release trapped trauma" | **Prohibited** — unsupported physiological release claim |
| "preventing it from trapping in the body as trauma" | **Prohibited** — unsupported causal claim |

### What replaces them

The four angles already established: **the facilitated deliverable · privacy by
architecture · verifiable qualification · the refusal itself.** Sector-specific
recognition — naming the experience accurately — does the persuasive work that
outcome claims would otherwise do, and it stays inside the rules.
