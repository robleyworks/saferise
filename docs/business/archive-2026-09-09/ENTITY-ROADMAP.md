## Position today

SafeRise operates under **Kenor International B.V.** (registered, Sint Maarten).
That entity can already:

- take payment
- hold the platform IP
- issue a convertible promissory note
- sign B2B and corporate contracts

**Nothing in the 60-day plan requires a new entity.** Incorporating now would
consume the entire tranche and delay launch without adding capability.

---

## Why separate at all — the actual reasons

Not "because startups have companies." These are the conditions where staying
inside Kenor starts to cost something:

| Reason | Bites when |
|---|---|
| **Cap table contamination** | Kenor holds or acquires activities unrelated to SafeRise. An investor buying into SafeRise does not want exposure to them, and diligence gets expensive |
| **Multiple holders of conversion rights** | Once more than one person has a claim on future equity, informal arrangements stop being workable |
| **Equity for people who aren't you** | A co-founder, advisor, or collaborator taking a stake needs a share class and a vesting instrument |
| **Investor requirement** | Term sheets frequently name the entity they will fund into |
| **Liability ring-fencing** | A wellness platform carries a different risk profile from Kenor's other activity, whatever that is |

None of these are live yet. That is why this is a roadmap, not a task.

## The decision that must come first

**Decide the target structure before incorporating anything.**

If a Sint Maarten B.V. is formed now and the month-7 pre-seed comes from US or
EU investors who require a familiar holding structure, the unwind is expensive:
share transfers, potential tax on transferring the IP out, legal fees on both
sides, and a delay at exactly the wrong moment.

**Three candidate structures**

| Structure | Fits when | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Stay in Kenor International B.V. | Bootstrapped, one note holder, no external equity | Cheapest by far. Viable well past launch |
| New Sint Maarten B.V. | Local operation, local/regional investors, revenue supports compliance | Local presence, resident-director requirement already satisfied |
| Foreign holdco over a local operating entity | Institutional pre-seed from US/EU | Most expensive; only justified once a term sheet is real |

**Action:** ○ Put this question to counsel at the session already budgeted in the
60-day forecast. It costs nothing extra to ask while they're drafting the note.
It costs a great deal to ask after the fact.

## Consequence for the note being raised now

The note converts into shares of **whichever entity exists at conversion**. If
SafeRise later moves entity, the holder's conversion right has to travel with it.

**Must be drafted in from the start:**
- ○ Successor-entity / reorganisation clause — conversion right survives a
  restructure or redomicile
- ○ Conversion trigger — priced round, any raise, or a date backstop
- ○ Discount and/or valuation cap
- ⚠ Currency — USD or EUR
- ⚠ Structure — one-off, or monthly for a defined period

Without the successor clause, a future restructure either strands the holder or
forces a renegotiation from a weak position. This is the single most important
drafting point on a small note.

## Sint Maarten mechanics — for when the trigger fires

### Sequence

1. **Civil-law notary** — deed of incorporation and articles of association.
   The notary is the entry point; N.V. and B.V. cannot be formed without one.
2. **Business licence** — Department of Economic Licenses, Ministry of TEATT
   (*Vestigingsvergunning*), plus a director's licence (*Directeursvergunning*).
3. **Chamber of Commerce & Industry (COCI)** — registration in the Commercial
   Register. The notary usually handles this.
4. **UBO registration** — beneficial-owner filing. Where a notary executed the
   deed, the notary makes the declaration.
5. **CRIB number** — Tax Administration Office, Ministry of Finance, for tax
   filing.
6. **SZV** — only if there are employees.

### Indicative cost

| Item | NAƒ / Cg | ≈ USD | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Notary — deed and articles | quote | 1,500–3,000 | ⚠ Varies with authorised capital and complexity. Get two quotes |
| Business licence, NV/BV | 1,550 | ~866 | Higher than the non-NV/BV rate of 312.50 |
| Director's licence, single director | 312.50 | ~175 | 625 if more than one director |
| Application processing fee | 150 | ~84 | Paid at submission |
| COCI registration | varies | — | Scales with paid-up capital |
| **One-time total** | | **~2,700–4,200** | |
| Annual — COCI fee, accounting, filings | | ⚠ | Get an accountant's quote before committing |

**This exceeds the tranche by 2–4×.** It is a post-revenue decision, not a
pre-launch one.

### Constraints

- **Resident director required** — at least one director must be resident in
  Sint Maarten. Satisfied.
- **Timeline** — licensing runs weeks, not days. Budget six weeks minimum from
  application, longer if documents come back for correction.
- **Tax** — profit tax is reported around 34.5%, plus turnover tax on local
  revenue. How TOT applies to subscription revenue from non-resident members is
  a question for a local accountant, not an assumption to make.

## Gate C — triggers incorporation

Do not incorporate on a date. Incorporate when **any one** of these is true:

| # | Trigger | Status |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Signed LOI or term sheet naming a required entity | ○ |
| C2 | A second party holds conversion rights | ○ |
| C3 | A co-founder, advisor, or collaborator is taking equity | ○ |
| C4 | MRR ≥ €1,500 — revenue can carry annual compliance | ○ |
| C5 | A corporate B2B contract requires a distinct counterparty | ○ |
| C6 | Kenor takes on activity that would contaminate a SafeRise cap table | ○ |

**Realistic timing.** C4 at €1,500 MRR is roughly 60–80 Track 01 members, or a
smaller mixed base once Tracks 02 and 03 open. On the current plan that lands
somewhere past month 4 — which sits naturally ahead of the month-7 raise, with
enough margin to incorporate without it becoming the thing holding up a term
sheet.

deal slows down — and never a cost carried before there is revenue to carry it.
