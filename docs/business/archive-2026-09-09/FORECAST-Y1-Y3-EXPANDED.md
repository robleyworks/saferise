## The finding that matters

**Run the model with the distribution problem never solved — free signups flat at
Year 1 levels for three years — and Year 3 is still €471,154.**

| Year | B2B share of revenue |
|---|---|
| Y1 | 16% |
| Y2 | **37%** |
| Y3 | **53%** |

The earlier forecast collapsed without distribution, because it was
subscription-led and subscription depends on an input that does not yet exist.

**With vertical tracks and two B2B markets, the business survives its own biggest
risk.** B2B licence revenue does not require free signups, SEO, ad spend or an
audience. It requires a named buyer, a quantified pain and a sales motion — all
three of which are already in hand.

That is the single strongest argument in the whole plan, and it only becomes true
once industry-specific tracks exist.

## Why vertical tracks change B2B economics

A generic wellbeing track is a benefit. An industry-specific track is a solution.

| | Generic track | Vertical track |
|---|---|---|
| What it is to the buyer | Nice-to-have wellbeing | An intervention against a named, costed problem |
| Who the buyer is | HR, discretionary budget | Operations, with a line item already open |
| Sales cycle | 3–9 months | 6–12 weeks |
| **Average contract value, Europe** | **€6,000** | **€15,000** |
| Delivery cost | Identical | Identical |
| Comparison set | Every wellbeing app | Almost nothing |

**A Contact Centre track is not wellbeing. It is an attrition intervention.**
Attrition is measured monthly in that sector, carries a known cost per head, and
already has budget attached. You would not be creating a category — you would be
entering a funded one with a product nobody else has built.

The model reflects this: European ACV rises from €6,000 to €11,000 to €15,000 as
verticals ship.

## Per-track economics

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Build effort | ~195 hours — 10 protocols, ~50,000 words, 10 covers, 10 posters, 10 recordings |
| Replacement cost | ~$16,350 |
| **Cash cost if contracted** | **~$6,500** |
| Return, generic track | +€1.60 blended ARPU per member per month |
| Return, vertical track | Unlocks a named segment. Three licences at €15,000 repays it **7×** |

**A vertical track pays for itself on the first licence and funds the next two
tracks on the second.**

## The asymmetry that should decide capital allocation

| Input | Can money buy it reliably? |
|---|---|
| **More tracks** | **Yes.** A contracted writer converts cash into finished tracks predictably |
| **Free signups** | **Not yet.** No channel is proven. Ad spend into an unindexed site with no audience is an experiment, not a purchase |

**Spend on tracks before spending on acquisition.** Tracks feed the line that
does not depend on the unsolved problem, and each vertical opens a segment that
can be sold with skills already in the building.

Revisit once a channel has a proven cost per signup — then acquisition becomes a
purchase rather than a bet.

## Industry tracks — build order

Ranked by population × pain × budget × access.

| # | Track | Workforce | Why it ranks here | Build |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Contact Centre** | BPO agents | Attrition already measured and costed. Tens of thousands of seats in Jamaica and the DR alone. English-operating | Y2 Q1 |
| 2 | **Frontline Hospitality** | Front-of-house, resorts | Largest regional employer. Required to perform warmth while absorbing hostility | Y2 Q2 |
| 3 | **Clinical Staff** | Nurses, allied health | Burnout is named, unstigmatised, and retention is existential | Y2 Q4 |
| 4 | **Shift & Night Work** | Utilities, ports, airports, logistics | Circadian disruption plus safety-critical decisions. Nobody serves this | Y3 Q1 |
| 5 | **First Responders** | Emergency services | Highest resonance of any vertical. ⚠ Also the highest clinical-governance bar — needs a licensed reviewer before drafting | Y3 Q2 |
| 6 | **Educators** | Teachers, school staff | High need, low budget. Grant-funded route. Good for references | Y3 Q3 |

**Consumer tracks continue in parallel** — Training & The Body, Sex & Intimacy,
Elevation, Money Mindset, Addiction Recovery — because they feed B2C ARPU and
give the free tier new entry points.

⚠ **First Responders must not be built under commercial pressure.** It is the
vertical with the most exposure to trauma content and the clinical-governance gap
is still open. It stays behind a licensed clinical reviewer.

### ⚠ The margin is overstated and must be presented that way

**Founder time costs nothing in this model.** Draw a market salary and Year 1 is
negative, Year 2 comfortable, Year 3 genuinely profitable.

State it before anyone asks. A service-derived business showing 80% margin
invites exactly one question, and answering it first is worth more than the
number.

## What has to be true

| Assumption | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| 12 tracks shipped by M36 | **Medium.** Solo build velocity is ~1 track per 6–8 weeks while operating | Contracted writers from Y2. This is the cash-solvable constraint |
| 29 European licences over 3 years | **Medium.** ~10/year at 3–9 month cycles is a full seller's load | It is a full seller's load, and the founder is a full seller. Second seller in Y3 |
| Vertical ACV reaches €15,000 | **Medium.** Untested. No vertical track exists yet | First Contact Centre licence proves or disproves it. Test early |
| B2C free funnel grows | **High.** Still the unbuilt input | The sensitivity above shows the business stands without it |
| Delivery stays capped | **Low.** Deliberate | Facilitator from Y3 |

**The forecast's largest single risk is no longer distribution.** It is build
velocity — and build velocity is the one constraint that money reliably fixes.
