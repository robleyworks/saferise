Gate A · month six

The four numbers that decide which raise is available.

Ratios first, volume second. At this scale a backer is not testing whether we can buy users — they are testing whether the unit economics hold when we do. 250 members at 4% churn beats 800 at 12%.

MetricFloorNowWhy this number
Free → paid conversion≥ 3.5%— Below 3% the funnel is the problem, not the reach. Path B assumes 4.2%; at 2% you raise Path A and fix the funnel first
Monthly churn, paid≤ 6%— Above 6% annualises past 50% and every forecast built on it collapses
Second-protocol open rate≥ 45%— Whether the method transfers between states — which is the whole architecture
Paying members≥ 1800 The floor at which conversion and churn are statistically meaningful rather than anecdotal

Tier 2, which makes the conversation easy: 300+ members · €6,500+ MRR · 4,000+ free accounts · ≥20% on the €29 tier · ≥30% thirty-day free retention · two written pilot findings reports · one named reference customer. None of these are measurable until instrumentation is live — and retrofitting it in month four loses the first cohort entirely.

Open

Three items still open.

Founder salary — €70,000 to €90,000, start date undecided

The model carries zero, which is what makes Path A add up. Path A raises €350,000 against a €238,000 trough; with the stated 12% contingency that leaves roughly €83,000 of headroom, and that headroom is the entire room available for a salary without re-raising. It buys 14 months at €70,000, 12 at €80,000, 11 at €90,000 — none of which reach breakeven in year three.

So the live question is when it starts, not what it is. Paid from close through to breakeven, Path A has to rise to roughly €407,000 at €70,000, €427,000 at €80,000, €447,000 at €90,000 — and at that point it sits close enough to Path B's €500,000 that the trade the deck presents on slides 37 and 38 stops being a real choice. Starting at month thirteen on €70,000 costs about €84,000 to breakeven and fits the existing €350,000 almost exactly.
