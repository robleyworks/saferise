# SafeRise B2B — readiness assessment and coaching-as-a-service packaging

**13 September 2026.** Written to substantiate the coaching-as-a-service position, not to
argue against it.

---

## 1 · The argument, taken seriously

The objection to calling this coaching is that coaching means a person in a room. That
objection assumes the human *is* the method. In most coaching it is — the coach carries
the framework in their head and delivers it conversationally, which is why it costs what
it costs and why quality varies by who you get.

**SafeRise inverts that.** The method is written down, sequenced, produced and identical
every time. The human is a layer on top, not the mechanism.

That is not less than coaching. It is coaching with the variance removed — and the thing
that makes it defensible is that **the instructional payload is real**:

| Layer | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Guided implementation** | A complete sequenced session — Recognise, Regulate, Release, Rise — not content about a method but the method being run |
| **Somatic release** | Physical intervention when cognition is not available. Most coaching has no answer here at all |
| **Explanation** | Mechanism, not instruction. The person understands why the sequence moves in this order |
| **Perspectives** | Multiple interpretive frames offered without prescribing one — closer to supervision than to content |
| **Cue cards** | Transfer into the live moment. The thing coaching most often fails at |
| **Disclosure and invitation** | Language for the actual conversation with another person |
| **Journal and capacity check** | Reflective practice, structured, private |

**Seven modalities around one situation.** That is a coaching curriculum by any honest
reading. A human coach delivering all seven for one presenting issue would take four to
six sessions.

**Where the claim is strongest:** attention governance and state regulation are
*upstream* competencies. They are what coaching, training and L&D all assume and none
teach. Owning the upstream capability is a stronger B2B position than owning another
downstream outcome.

## 2 · Honest readiness assessment

Scored against what a mid-market or enterprise buyer actually asks for.

| Dimension | State | Gap to enterprise-ready |
|---|---|---|
| **Method** | Strong | None. Named, sequenced, written, consistent |
| **Content depth** | Strong | 30 protocols live, seven resource types each |
| **Delivery mechanism** | Strong | Self-serve, no scheduling constraint, scales to any headcount |
| **Human layer** | **Thin** | Workshops and 1:1 exist as line items, not as a staffed capability |
| **Clinical governance** | **Open gate** | Escalation criteria drafted, not agreed. Blocks any live sale |
| **Evidence of outcome** | **Absent** | No pilot data, no case study, no named reference |
| **Buyer-facing artefacts** | **Absent** | No one-pager, no security page, no DPA, no ROI frame |
| **Procurement readiness** | **Absent** | No SSO, no admin console, no invoicing path above card payment |
| **Reporting** | **Committed, unbuilt** | Quarterly seats-in-use promised; nothing produces the figure |
| **Reference customers** | **Zero** | No organisation has paid anything |

**The method is enterprise-grade. The wrapper is not.** That is the whole assessment in
one line — and it is a far better problem to have than the reverse.

## 3 · What actually blocks the sale

Not the price. Not the category label. Four things, in order of severity.

**1 · No evidence.** Every B2B wellbeing buyer has been burned by an app with a low
engagement rate. The first question is "what happened when someone else did this", and
there is currently no answer.

**2 · No clinical governance.** Any organisation putting this in front of employees will
ask what happens when someone is in crisis. Escalation criteria are drafted and unagreed.
**Nothing live should be sold until they are.**

**3 · No procurement surface.** SSO, an admin view, invoicing, a DPA, a security page.
Each one alone is a reason for a deal to stall for a quarter.

**4 · No human capacity.** Coaching-as-a-service is a promise of contact. The current
bench is roughly four hours a month.

⚠ **Point 4 is the one that determines pricing.** Everything else is buildable on a
laptop. Facilitator hours are not.

## 4 · The packaging

Three tiers, structured so **the human layer scales with the price** and the promise never
exceeds the bench.

### Tier 1 — **Foundation** · SMB, 25–150 seats

**€9,000 / year** · from €60 per seat, sliding to €36 at 150

- Full platform, every track, every protocol
- Onboarding session — 90 minutes, remote, delivered live
- **One facilitated group session per quarter** (4 per year)
- Quarterly seats-in-use report
- Email support

*Facilitator cost: ~6 hours a year per account.*

### Tier 2 — **Practice** · mid-market, 150–750 seats

**€38,000 / year** · from €50 per seat, sliding to €25

- Everything in Foundation
- **One facilitated session per month** (12 per year)
- A half-day programme for people managers, annually
- Named contact, quarterly review call
- SSO, admin console, DPA

*Facilitator cost: ~22 hours a year per account.*

### Tier 3 — **Partner** · enterprise, 750+

**From €95,000 / year** · scoped

- Everything in Practice
- **Fortnightly facilitated sessions across multiple cohorts**
- Two in-person days annually
- Manager enablement programme
- Protocol adaptation for their context
- Executive reporting and an annual review

*Facilitator cost: ~60 hours a year per account. **This tier requires a second
facilitator.***

---

### Why these numbers

**€36–60 per seat for Foundation** and €25–50 for Practice. Well below coaching-panel
pricing, well above app pricing — which is the correct place to sit, because you are
delivering more than an app and less than one-to-one human hours.

**The delivered layer is what makes the price defensible.** Take the facilitated sessions
out and this is a €15-a-seat licence. Put them in and it is a service with a method behind
it. **The sessions are the product**; the platform is what makes them stick between them.

**Each tier is capacity-sized.** Foundation at six hours a year means ten accounts cost 60
hours annually — deliverable by one person. Practice at 22 means five accounts is 110
hours. Partner needs a second facilitator before the first one is signed.

⚠ **Do not sell Tier 3 until a second facilitator exists.** One in-person day with a
conflict is a broken contract.

## 5 · What the pilot becomes

The current €1,800 pilot is priced as an event. It should be priced as **the qualifying
step into Foundation**.

> **Pilot — €4,500.** A half-day delivered programme for up to three groups, plus 25
> platform seats for 90 days, plus a written findings report. **Credited in full against a
> Foundation contract signed within 60 days.**

**Why this is better than €1,800:** it is a real commitment so it attracts real buyers, it
produces the findings document that becomes your first case study, and the credit makes
the full contract feel like a decision already half-made.

**The findings report is the important artefact.** Anonymous, aggregate, honest — where
people were before, what they used, what shifted, what did not. Two or three of those and
the evidence gap in §3 closes.

## 6 · The sales case

### The problem, stated to a buyer

> Your people already have support. An EAP nobody calls, training they attended once,
> managers told to have better conversations. None of it reaches them in the moment that
> actually costs you — the meeting that goes badly, the conversation avoided for three
> weeks, the decision deferred again.
>
> In a triggered state, everything you have given them is unreachable.

### The claim

> SafeRise teaches the capability that makes the rest of it land: recognising the state,
> regulating it, and recovering the ability to choose. It is delivered as guided sessions
> people run themselves, with facilitated group work that turns it into a shared
> practice.

### Why it beats the alternatives

| They have | Why it does not reach the moment |
|---|---|
| **EAP** | Reactive, stigmatised, single-digit utilisation |
| **Coaching panel** | Expensive, rationed to leadership, scheduled weeks out |
| **Meditation app** | General calm, no situational specificity, engagement collapses |
| **Resilience training** | One session, no transfer, nothing to return to |

**The line that lands:** *Every one of those assumes the person can access their own
judgement. Ours is what gets them there.*

### The ROI frame — use carefully

Do not promise numbers you cannot evidence. Frame the cost side and let them do the
arithmetic:

> One avoided resignation at mid-level costs an organisation somewhere between six and
> nine months of salary to replace. Foundation costs less than a fortnight of one.

That is honest, it is checkable, and it does not claim an outcome you have not measured.

## 7 · Build order

**Before any B2B outreach:**

1. **Agree the escalation criteria.** Hard gate, blocks everything
2. **Answer the insurance and permission question** for whoever delivers
3. **Verify seat reporting is deliverable** — it is a written commitment
4. **Remove the noindex** and fix remaining price inconsistencies

**Before a mid-market conversation:**

5. One-page overview, security and data page, DPA
6. SSO and a basic admin console
7. Invoicing above card payment

**To close the evidence gap:**

8. **Run two pilots at cost.** The findings reports are worth more than the fee
9. One named reference, even a small one

**Before Tier 3:**

10. **A second facilitator.** Not optional

## 8 · The honest position

**The method substantiates the claim. The operation does not yet.**

Coaching-as-a-service is defensible here — not because of what competitors charge, but
because seven instructional modalities around a named, sequenced framework *is* a coaching
curriculum, and the delivered sessions in every tier make it a service rather than a
licence.

What is missing is not the argument. It is **evidence, governance and capacity** — and
those are the three things a buyer will test in the first meeting.

⚠ **The single biggest risk is selling Tier 2 or 3 before the bench exists.** A missed
facilitated session in month three of a €38,000 contract is worse than never having sold
it. Size the pipeline to the hours, and grow the hours before the pipeline.
