## 3 · Track 09 — women's track, draft scope

Organised by **state**, not population — matching how Tracks 01–03 work. Track 01
is not "for anxious people", it is ten states.

The unifying thread is not womanhood. It is that all these groups are told the
same thing: that what they are experiencing is character, hormones, or being
difficult. That is the existing SafeRise thesis with a specific audience.

1. The rage that arrives from nowhere
2. The vigilant night — 3am waking, wired and exhausted
3. Sensory overload — noise, heat, touch, light
4. Masking collapse — the shutdown once the door closes
5. Flooding — emotion outpacing thought
6. The competence drop — fog, word-finding, fear of diminishing
7. The unfamiliar body
8. Depletion without a floor
9. Being disbelieved — the loop after being told it is stress
10. Going unseen

Population-specific material lives in the **resources**, not the protocols.

### Why perimenopause is the strongest single idea

Kirsten already works in it. The autonomic case is real rather than borrowed —
oestrogen decline affects vagal tone, thermoregulation and sleep architecture.
The rage from nowhere, the 3am waking, the sudden intolerance of noise are
dysregulation rather than mood. Women experiencing it are routinely told it is
stress or depression, which is precisely the recognition failure the platform
exists to address. Commercially the category is HRT on one side and supplements
on the other, with almost nothing addressing state.

**Neurodivergent women** is the other strong candidate — masking as the
mechanism, late-diagnosed women told for decades they are too sensitive or too
much. Must be **co-developed rather than adapted**; that audience is well
informed and allergic to neurotypical advice with a label on it. Needs autistic
and ADHD reviewers, not only Kirsten.

### ⚠ Excluded from version one

**Maternal regulation** — postpartum rage, touch-overwhelm, sensory load. Real
gap, highest acute risk on the board, because postpartum depression and psychosis
sit immediately beside it. Not to be built without a licensed clinician already
in place. Deserves its own track later.

**Caregiver depletion** — large population, honest autonomic story, weaker fit
with Kirsten's stated expertise.

### ⚠ Highest-risk protocol

**Number 9, "being disbelieved."** A protocol about medical dismissal could,
written carelessly, keep someone away from care they need. It needs escalation
criteria written by someone licensed before a word is drafted.

Known escalation territory: thyroid disease, cardiac symptoms, postpartum
psychosis, major depression.

## 4 · The working model

Full version in `TRACK-PROGRAMME-MODEL.md`.

**Four roles, non-overlapping.** Founder holds architecture and voice.
Collaborator holds substantiation and sourcing. Clinician holds safety.
Lived-experience readers hold whether it lands.

**Six stages.** Scoping → **escalation sheet** → outline plus one written
protocol → substantiation → clinical review → build and ship.

**Escalation sheet before writing, not after.** Safety boundaries change what a
protocol *is*. Discovered at significant build they cause rewrites, not
annotations. The clinician's first paid task is reviewing ten pages rather than
15,000 words — the cheapest possible way to buy a clinician's signature.

**Handover at outline plus one, not at significant build.** By the time material
feels substantial it is fixed — voice set, resources structured, sequencing
committed. A reviewer arriving then can only approve or cause expensive damage,
and most choose approve. That produces sign-off without scrutiny, which is worse
than no review because it looks like the good outcome.

**Total unavoidable cash: 3–6 hours of licensed clinical time per track.**

⚠ **The clinician contracts to Kenor International B.V., not to Kirsten.** She
finds and briefs them; the engagement letter, scope, payment and sign-off are
Andre's. If the relationship runs through her it dissolves the moment her health
or priorities shift.

⚠ **Sourcing expertise through Kirsten does not move liability.** Kenor publishes
it, Kenor carries it. A referral chain buys better content and better reviewers —
it is not a liability structure.

## 6 · The MOU — next artifact

One memorandum of understanding as a template, then replicated for Dwight,
Aleksandra, further contributors, and eventually financiers.

- **Role title and the exact public description**, including how credentials
  appear on the site, word for word
- **Scope of contribution** — what they deliver
- **Explicit exclusions** — what they do not decide, sign off, or represent
- **Credential representation clause** — what may and may not be claimed publicly
  on either side
- **IP and ownership** — unambiguously
- **Compensation** — revenue share, deferred, stipend or equity, agreed in
  writing before work starts
- **Load and availability expectations**, stated honestly
- **Term, exit and what happens to the work on exit**
- **Confidentiality**, tied to the founder's-account handling rules
- **Governing law** — Kenor International B.V., Sint Maarten

**This is a legal document and needs a lawyer.** Same engagement as the transfer
mechanism and the gendered-positioning advertising review. Claude can draft the
commercial substance and scoping language; it should not be the final instrument.

**Why now:** two people have already turned out to be scoped wrong — Bastien's
credential and Kirsten's role. The document that prevents a third is short.

## 8 · Working rules carried forward

**Do not research collaborators from the open web.** Credentials come from the
person, in writing, verified by Andre against the issuing register's own site —
not a directory, not their website. This is the check that was skipped with
Bastien.

**Verify before asserting.** Four errors in two days, all the same shape: stating
state without checking it. Same discipline applies to people.

**Nothing depicted as broken.** Applies to gendered content with particular
force — perimenopause and neurodivergence are both routinely framed as deficits,
and SafeRise must not.

**No outcome promises. No medical claims.** The gendered tracks sit closer to
medical claims than anything built so far. The line stays at regulation.

⚠ The footer claim is about the **field**, not the protocols. With Bastien
removed and no clinician in place, the statement that the protocols themselves
have not been clinically trialled must exist in `terms.html` before this deploys.
