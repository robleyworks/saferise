# B2B documents — lineage, and the points still open

The B2B papers are a **progressive record**: each one develops the programme
further, so where they differ the later one usually supersedes the earlier. This
note fixes the order, records what superseded what, and separates that from the
handful of points where something genuinely still needs a decision.

Checked 22 September against `organisations.html` rather than argued from
preference.

---

## The order

| | Document | What it settled |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Development & packaging framework** | Where the Role-versus-Industry split was worked out. Carries the fullest per-protocol detail: audience, territory, resource lanes, scores. |
| 2 | **Architecture conversation** (Retail, Foodservice, Security, Sport) | Argued the four additions in, and the move from "Department" to "Role / Function". Longer descriptive names — superseded. |
| 3 | **Handover** | Condensed to the short R01–R14 / I01–I16 names, added the visual system, casting and boundaries. Marked as the single source of direction. |
| 4 | **v6 Executive Summary & Programme Breakdown** | The buyer-facing compression. |
| 5 | **Cover contact sheet** | Thirty covers, numbered, with taglines. |

The architecture is stable across all five — 8 + 2, fourteen Roles, sixteen
Industries. That part is settled and it is the part that matters.

## Settled by supersession — recorded so it does not get reintroduced

**The long names are dead.** "Leadership & People Management", "Owner, Founder &
Principal", "Security & Protective Operations", "Retail & In-Person Commerce",
"Restaurants, Foodservice & Nightlife", "Education, Childcare & Early
Development" all became the short forms in the handover. Worth stating, because
the earlier document reads authoritatively and someone will otherwise bring them
back.

Its reasoning is still the best statement of the whole idea and should survive
into the catalogue:

> Responsibility can be delegated. Ultimate exposure often cannot.

**Two anchors moved.** R02 went "Ownership Weight" → "The Weight of Ownership";
I13 went "Competition Recovery" → "Competition to Recovery" — or the reverse,
depending which of the framework and the handover you date later. The framework
calls "The Weight of Ownership" one of the strongest titles developed, and it is
the better line. **Pick, then freeze** — an anchor is the protocol's product
name and it cannot be approximate across the page, the covers and the catalogue.

## Still open

### 1 · The Foundation 8 is named wrongly in the newest document

This is the one that matters, and it is not evolution — it is the most recent,
most buyer-facing document disagreeing with the shipped product.

| # | Live site & handover | v6 Executive Summary & framework |
|---|---|---|
| 05 | **Embodied Nutrition** | "Nutrition" |
| 06 | **Strength & Return** | "Fitness" |

`organisations.html` renders exactly:

```
Capacity     Personal Transformation      Application  Executive Presence
Relational   Relationship Healing         Substrate    Embodied Nutrition
Application  Professional Performance     Substrate    Strength & Return
Beyond       Elevation Series             Substrate    Sleep & Recovery
```

"Fitness" and "Nutrition" are the generic wellness words the positioning is
built to avoid. And a buyer who reads the summary, then opens the product and
sees different track names, starts wondering what else is approximate.

**Correct the summary to the product names.** Five minutes, highest value here.

### 2 · Development status is invisible in the summary

The Executive Summary lists all eight tracks flat, with no status. Three are
live; five are in development. An enterprise buyer will read that list as eight
live tracks.

The site already handles this properly with restrained chips. The summary needs
the same honesty — this is exactly what diligence finds.

Sub-point: the handover marks four in development (05–08); the site shows five
and says in prose that Executive Presence is among them. The site is almost
certainly right.

### 3 · The scorecard undercuts itself

The summary scores five criteria at **5/5 across the board**. A scorecard where
everything is full marks is not an assessment, and a professional reader
discounts the whole page. The framework's own spread — 8.7 to 9.9 — is far more
credible precisely because it is not uniform.

One protocol, I02 Emergency Response, is scored a perfect **10**. Nothing should
be a 10 in what the handover itself calls "an editorial / internal commercial
assessment, not scientific efficacy."

**Use the framework's /10 spread, drop the perfect score, label it as internal
editorial.**

### 4 · The cover taglines sit in a different voice

"Care Always", "Brighter Futures", "What's Next", "Stronger Teams". These are
aspirational brand lines. SafeRise's voice everywhere else is about states, not
aspiration — "Care without asking people to disappear inside it".

They are also doing a job the anchor already does better: "Safer People. Safer
Places." against "Vigilance Without Carryover" — the second is the one that
makes somebody in that job feel recognised.

If they are baked into artwork they can stand there. **Keep them off the website
cards**, where the anchor is available.

### 5 · Contact Centres is missing and was rated highly

The architecture conversation put Contact Centres & Customer Operations on the
second-wave bench but noted it "may actually deserve early prioritisation". The
occupational case is strong and distinct: queue pressure, monitoring, metrics,
no control over volume, repeated customer distress, seconds of recovery between
calls.

On the framework's own criteria it looks stronger than Real Estate or Events,
both of which made the sixteen. Worth a decision rather than leaving it as an
oversight.

## What I would do, in order

1. Correct **Embodied Nutrition** and **Strength & Return** in the Executive
   Summary and framework.
2. Add development status wherever the Foundation 8 is listed for a buyer.
3. Replace the all-5/5 block with the framework's /10 spread; remove the 10/10;
   label it internal editorial.
4. Freeze the two anchors.
5. Decide on Contact Centres.
6. Keep the cover taglines off the website cards.
