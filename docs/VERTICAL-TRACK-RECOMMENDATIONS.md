# TIER 1 · BUILD THESE

## 1 · Executive Presence — build first, and it is already written

**Score 24 · Dept · 65% reuse · €18,000 ACV · cheapest build in the portfolio**

Ten protocols are already scoped in `docs/EXECUTIVE-PRESENCE-PROSPECTUS-v3.md`.

```
01  Self-Disqualification        06  Asking for More
02  The First Hire               07  Letting Someone Go
03  Peer to Boss                 08  Carrying What They Told You
04  Losing the Craft             09  What It's Costing at Home
05  The Promised Number          10  Role Drift
```

**Coverage.** The transitions of gaining authority — being seen as a candidate,
becoming responsible for other people's livelihoods, losing the craft that got
you promoted, and the private cost nobody at work sees.

**Why first.** It is written. It has the highest ACV in the portfolio and the
cheapest build. Leadership development budgets are the largest and least
questioned line in any organisation, and they are not wellbeing budgets — which
means it clears procurement as development, not as mental health.

⚠ **The one risk:** protocols 1, 9 and 10 are exactly the ones a corporate buyer
would ask you to soften. *What It's Costing at Home* names the family paying for
the promotion. **Do not soften them** — they are why a leader trusts it.

## 2 · Sales & Quota — the highest-authority track available

**Score 24 · Dept · 55% reuse · €14,000 ACV · 0.8-month payback**

Ten protocols:

```
01  Quota Weight              06  Deal Collapse
02  Rejection Load            07  The Silence
03  The Forecast Call         08  Pre-Concession
04  Month End                 09  The Ramp
05  Leaderboard Exposure      10  Carrying the Number Home
```

| Protocol | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Quota Weight** | Carrying a number as a permanent background condition, not an event |
| **Rejection Load** | What accumulates from being told no forty times a week, and why it stops being survivable around month four |
| **The Forecast Call** | Being asked to predict, then judged on the prediction. Dread that arrives the night before |
| **Month End** | The compression. Three weeks of work in five days, every month, for a career |
| **Leaderboard Exposure** | Public ranking among peers you have to keep working with |
| **Deal Collapse** | A quarter of work gone in one email, and having to open the next one |
| **The Silence** | Ghosting. The specific state of no answer, which is worse than a no |
| **Pre-Concession** | Discounting yourself in your own head before the buyer asks. The internal cave |
| **The Ramp** | New territory, empty pipeline, everyone watching, and no results possible yet |
| **Carrying the Number Home** | It does not stop at six. What the number does to a Sunday |

**Alignment: 5.** Nothing needs inventing. Every one of these is a state the
existing method already handles — this is the same instrument pointed at a
specific working life.

**Why it is the strongest strategic build.** You carried a number for a decade
and delivered €2.6M ARR. A wellness founder writing *Rejection Load* gets it
wrong in the first paragraph and every salesperson reading it knows immediately.
**That authority does not transfer to any other track in the portfolio.**

**Convert: 5.** Sales enablement budget, one decision-maker, weeks not quarters,
and it never touches wellbeing procurement.

⚠ **Depth: 4, not 5.** The existing twelve resources mostly carry, but this track
wants a thirteenth — a **ninety-second pre-call reset**, because a salesperson
between calls has ninety seconds and not eleven minutes. Budget that as new
resource design, not adaptation.

## 3 · Contact Centre — the largest populations, and the home region

**Score 22 · Ind · 50% reuse · €15,000 ACV · Scale and Enterprise bands**

Ten protocols:

```
01  Absorbed Hostility        06  Repetition Fatigue
02  The Next Call             07  Shift Disruption
03  Performed Warmth          08  Queue Dread
04  Measured by the Second    09  Always Recorded
05  The Escalation            10  Leaving It at the Door
```

| Protocol | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Absorbed Hostility** | Being shouted at by a stranger for something you did not do |
| **The Next Call** | No recovery interval. The system does not care that the last one was bad |
| **Performed Warmth** | Producing warmth you do not feel, for eight hours. The specific exhaustion of emotional labour |
| **Measured by the Second** | Adherence, handle time, after-call work. Being quantified continuously |
| **The Escalation** | When it goes above you, and what that says about you |
| **Repetition Fatigue** | The fortieth identical call, and the flatness that follows |
| **Shift Disruption** | Rotating shifts, and a body that never settles into a rhythm |
| **Queue Dread** | The anticipatory state between calls, which is often worse than the call |
| **Always Recorded** | Permanent observation as a working condition |
| **Leaving It at the Door** | The commute, and what arrives home with you |

**Market: 5.** Jamaica's BPO sector alone employs tens of thousands. Sites run
500–3,000 seats. **Attrition is measured monthly, costed per head, and already
funded** — you are entering an existing budget category rather than creating one.

**Convert: 5.** Relationship-led procurement, 2–8 week cycles, English-operating,
and it is your own region.

⚠ **Depth: 3 — the lowest of the three, and the reason this is third not first.**
Contact-centre reality breaks two architectural assumptions. There is no private
space, and there are no eleven minutes. **This track needs a between-calls format
of sixty to ninety seconds** and a version that works with a supervisor nearby.
That is genuine new design, not adaptation, and it is the real cost of this
track.

# TIER 2 · AFTER ONE LICENCE HAS SOLD

Do not start these until the ACV assumption has been tested against a real
signature.

**Customer Support · 21 · 70% reuse.** The highest reuse in the portfolio and it
shares most states with Contact Centre. Build it as a variant of that track
rather than from scratch, and the marginal cost is small.

**HR & People Teams · 20 · not a revenue play.** Give the People function their
own track, free, before any licence conversation. They experience the product on
their own difficulty — absorbing everyone's distress with nobody holding theirs —
rather than evaluating it for someone else. **That is a better demonstration than
any deck**, and it costs one build.

**Engineering & On-Call · 19.** On-call is a genuinely distinct state — sleep
interrupted by an alert, a production incident with an audience, the blameless
post-mortem that still feels like blame. Depth of 3 because it needs a 3am
format.

**Legal & Compliance · 19.** High ACV, small populations. Adversarial by design,
privilege isolation, being the person who says no. Best sold alongside a licence
rather than standalone.

## Held behind clinical governance

**Clinical Staff (18)** and **First Responders (14)** both need a licensed
clinician contracted before drafting begins, not before shipping.

⚠ **First Responders has the highest payoff score in the portfolio and the lowest
depth.** That combination is the danger — maximum resonance, minimum
architectural fit, highest trauma exposure. **It is the track most likely to
cause harm if built under commercial pressure with an empty clinical layer.**
Neither enters the schedule until a clinician is contracted.

# THE ASSESSMENT THAT MATTERS MOST

## Resource enrichment depth — where the real cost hides

The portfolio forecast prices tracks by content reuse. **That understates the
cost for three of them**, because reuse measures whether the *protocols*
transfer, not whether the *resource architecture* does.

| Track | The architectural problem | New resource needed |
|---|---|---|
| **Contact Centre** | No private space, no eleven minutes | 60–90 second between-calls format |
| **Sales & Quota** | Ninety seconds between calls | Pre-call reset |
| **Engineering** | Woken at 3am by an alert | Incident-state format |
| **First Responders** | Regulation during, not after | Not solvable without clinical design |

**A new resource format is a platform change, not a track cost.** Build it once
for Sales and Contact Centre inherits it — which is another argument for building
those two adjacently.

## User benefit payoff — what the person actually gets

Payoff scored on how much one session returns *in that specific job*.

**Highest payoff: Contact Centre, Clinical Staff, First Responders, Educators,
Sales.** All five share one property — **the distress is continuous and the
person has no route out of it during the working day.** A ninety-second
regulation that works is transformative there in a way it is not for a lawyer
with a door that closes.

⚠ **Payoff and conversion are inversely correlated across this portfolio.** The
populations who benefit most — teachers, nurses, responders, agents — sit in the
organisations least able to pay. The populations who convert most easily —
executives, sales, legal — benefit least per session because they already have
options.

**That tension is worth naming rather than resolving.** The commercial answer is
to build the converting tracks and use them to fund the others. The honest answer
is to say so out loud rather than pretend the ranking is need-based.

⚠ **The pause at step two is the most important line in this document.** The
portfolio model assumes a vertical licence closes at €14,000–18,000 rather than
€6,000. No vertical track exists and none has been sold. **Committing €103,090 of
build capacity against that estimate is the one genuine mistake available here.**

If Sales & Quota closes three licences at €14,000, the model holds. If it closes
at €7,000, every figure halves and the build order should change — department
tracks still work, but it becomes a slower, smaller business than the table
implies.
