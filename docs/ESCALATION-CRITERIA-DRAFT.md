# Escalation criteria for live delivery

**Draft for review — Kirsten Morris**
13 September 2026 · SafeRise Protocol · Kenor International B.V.

---

## What this is, and what I'm asking you for

SafeRise is about to start selling live work — guided workshops, one-to-one sessions,
retreats and programmes delivered to organisations. Until now everything has been
self-guided audio, where a person is alone with a recording and can stop whenever they
want.

Live delivery is a different exposure. Someone can become distressed in front of a
facilitator, in a room with other people, in real time, and the facilitator has to decide
what to do in the next thirty seconds.

This document is the attempt to decide that in advance rather than in the moment.

**What I'm asking of you:** review it as the person who would actually be in the room.
Tell me where it's wrong, where it's naive, and where it asks a facilitator to do
something they shouldn't. I've written it from the platform's principles and from
ordinary care, not from clinical training. That gap is the reason it needs your eye.

Nothing here is fixed. Cut whatever should go.

---

## 1 · What SafeRise is, so the boundary is clear

Thirty self-guided sessions for the moments when anxiety, anger, overwhelm, conflict or
pressure start taking over. Four steps — Recognise, Regulate, Release, Rise. Breath work,
attention, and observation from a distance.

**It is not therapy, not counselling, and not a clinical intervention**, and it says so
throughout. The platform makes no diagnosis, treats no condition, and promises no
outcome. Its own materials repeatedly tell members that some states need another person
and that a practice is not a substitute for one.

Live delivery does not change what the work is. It changes only that someone is present
while it happens.

---

## 2 · The principle

**A facilitator's job in a live session is to run the protocol and to notice.**

Not to assess, not to treat, not to intervene clinically, and not to hold something the
session was never built to hold.

When something exceeds the work, the correct response is to stop the work and stay with
the person — not to go further with a better technique.

## 3 · Three tiers

### Tier one — inside the work

Ordinary and expected. No escalation.

- Tears, during or after
- Emotion arriving harder in the session than before it
- Someone stopping partway through
- Someone who feels nothing at all and says so
- Shaking, yawning, a sigh arriving on its own
- Tiredness afterwards, sometimes considerable
- Someone declining to do a step

**What the facilitator does:** carries on, or lets them stop. None of this is a problem
and treating it as one makes it one.

### Tier two — pause the session for this person

Something is happening that the protocol isn't the right tool for right now.

- A member becomes distressed and does not settle when the session moves on
- Something surfaces that is clearly larger than the day — an old loss, something from a
  long way back
- A member dissociates, goes blank, or cannot follow what is being said
- A panic response that escalates rather than easing with the breath
- A member discloses something serious mid-session — abuse, a bereavement, a crisis at
  home
- A member cannot stop crying and wants to continue anyway

**What the facilitator does:**

1. Stop the protocol for that person. The group can hold or break.
2. Stay with them. Ordinary presence, not technique.
3. Offer water, air, a different room. Reducing input is the first move, not the
   breathing.
4. Do not ask what it was about. Do not go further into it.
5. Do not leave them alone to recover.
6. Before they go: check they have someone to be with, and say plainly that what happened
   is ordinary and that talking to someone is a good idea.
7. Follow up within 24 hours.

⚠ **Kirsten — this is the section I am least sure about.** Particularly points 4 and 6.
Is "don't ask what it was about" right, or does it read as abandonment? And is a
24-hour follow-up appropriate or does it overstep?

### Tier three — stop and involve someone else, today

Beyond what any self-regulation practice is for.

- Any indication that a person may harm themselves
- Any indication that a person may harm someone else
- A member who appears to be losing contact with reality
- A member who appears intoxicated to a degree that makes participation unsafe
- A disclosure of current abuse, violence, or someone being at risk
- A medical event

**What the facilitator does:**

1. Stop. The session ends for that person and, if necessary, for everyone.
2. Stay with them. Do not send them home alone.
3. Contact emergency services where there is immediate danger.
4. Otherwise, help them contact someone today — their own doctor, a crisis line in their
   country, or a person they name.
5. Record what happened, factually, the same day.
6. Tell Andre the same day.

**The facilitator does not assess risk, does not decide whether someone is serious, and
does not counsel.** The job is to stop, stay, and hand over.

self-harm, or is that a clinical act we should not be doing? And where a member is an
employee attending a corporate session, does the employer get told anything at all? My
instinct is no, but a duty-of-care argument may cut the other way.

## 4 · Before any live session runs

- Every participant has seen the same advisory the platform carries, before they arrive
- Facilitators have this document, have read it, and have a crisis number for the country
  they are delivering in
- Nobody delivers alone at a retreat or a session over 20 people
- A quiet room or space to step out is available, always
- Participation in any step is optional and said out loud at the start

---

## 5 · Things we will not do

- Screen participants for mental health conditions. We are not qualified to and it would
  misrepresent what this is.
- Accept a referral from an employer, a GP or anyone else as though we were a treatment
  service.
- Deliver to a group identified as clinical or in crisis.
- Continue with someone who wants to stop.
- Promise any outcome, in a room or on a page.

---

## 6 · The honest limitation

SafeRise has no clinician. You are standing in, and that arrangement should be named
rather than implied.

Two things follow, and I'd like your view on both.

**What should we say publicly?** The site currently says this is not therapy. It does not
say who is behind the live work or what their qualifications are. Saying nothing is
defensible; saying something is better if what we say is true.

**Where is the line for you personally?** You are agreeing to be the person a facilitator
calls. That is a real commitment and it should be scoped — what you are and aren't
willing to be called about, and what happens when you are not available.
