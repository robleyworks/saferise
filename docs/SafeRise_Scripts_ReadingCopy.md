# SafeRise · Guided Meditation Scripts

Thirty protocols. Titles and script body only.

## T1-01 · Anxiety Reset

Something got triggered, and it got here before you did.

That's the usual order. The body goes first. The part of you that knows how to handle this comes second. It's slower, but it's there.

Most of the trouble lives in the gap between the two. The next few minutes close some of it.

Four steps. You don't need to keep track. I'll take you through them.

### Recognise

Where is it?

Chest. Throat. Stomach. You won't need to search. It's usually obvious.

Stay with it a moment. Notice what it's doing. Tight. Fast. Pushing.

Describe it in one word. Not what happened. What this is.

*Agitated.*

Notice where you were when you said it. Still in it. And for a second, also looking at it.

That second is what the rest of this is built on.

It gets quicker to find, the more you do this.

One layer down, if you want it. Underneath the word there's usually something the situation could cost you.

Often it's one of these.

Losing any say in how it goes.

Being left to deal with it alone.

Being the problem.

Running out of time for what you still mean to do.

If one landed closer, notice which. If none did, that's fine too.

Either way, leave it where it is. Nothing to solve here.

### Regulate

Now the breath. The count matters more than the size.

Breathe in for four.

And out for six. Longer out than in, every time.

Keep it small. A big breath takes effort, and effort pushes the wrong way. Long is what matters.

Bring your attention to the middle of your chest. Rest a hand there if that helps.

Keep the count going. In for four. Out for six.

Thoughts will come. Maybe a lot of them. That's what a mind does, and it doesn't mean this isn't working.

When one arrives, let it arrive. Don't push it away. Don't follow it. Don't finish it.

A thought turning up isn't a thought asking you to act.

If you notice yourself working hard at this, ease off. Watching takes no effort. Effort means you've started fighting.

Weather passes through a room. The room doesn't become the weather.

Stay here a little longer. This is the part people cut short.

### Release

Two things are going on right now, and only one of them is the feeling.

The other is the argument with it. *Not now.* *Not again.* *What's wrong with me.*

The argument costs more than the feeling does. And it's the only one of the two you can put down.

You don't have to like the feeling. Just stop spending yourself on the fact that it turned up.

Now change the question. Instead of *what's wrong with me*, ask:

*What was this for?*

This state moves ahead of thought. That's its job. It's why it doesn't check with you first.

You don't need the answer now. Keep the question. It's worth coming back to when you're steadier.

Some of this belongs to today. Some of it may be older. You don't have to sort which yet. Just notice there might be both.

Now the one that landed closest, if one did. Put it where your breath is, and leave it there.

If it was losing any say, you're right. Some of this isn't yours to control. How you go through it still is.

If it was being left alone, that fear is old, and it has its reasons. You're not deciding today who stays. That gets answered later, by what happens.

If it was being the problem, don't test it now. Picking at it from inside this state only makes it louder. Leave it where it is. And notice it didn't come from a good day.

If it was running out of time, that one's real. But nobody can look at something that size while their whole body is braced against it. Let go of the bracing. Not the fact.

None of it gets settled from inside this state. That's why it waits until you're out of it.

### Rise

Picture yourself from across the room, just triggered.

*This is a triggered moment. It isn't the whole of you.*

Now picture the version of you that you'd want to be in a moment like this. Same trigger. Same feeling rising. But you're the one deciding where your attention goes.

What do you notice first? What do you let pass? What do you decide matters?

Where are you? What's the first thing you say?

Now step into it. The feeling is still there. Your attention is where you put it.

Pick one thing you'll do this way next time.

Once that moment has happened, look back at it and write down what was different.

One more slow breath. In for four. Out for six.

Notice how you are now. Different or not, either is fine.

Move your hands and feet. Feel the floor under you. Open your eyes when you're ready.

Return to this guided meditation as often as you need to, until you no longer do.

## T1-02 · Anger Alchemy

Something got crossed. Not a mood, not a bad day — a line, and something went over it.

This one doesn't begin with settling. Anger doesn't settle on request, and asking it to is how these things fail in the first ten seconds. Keep moving if you're moving. Stay still if you're still.

Your position stays exactly where you're holding it. Nobody is going to talk you round.

That's the usual order. The body goes first. The part of you that knows how to handle this comes second. It's slower, but it's there.

Most of the trouble lives in the gap between the two. The next few minutes close some of it.

Four steps. You don't need to keep track. I'll take you through them.

### Recognise

Your body registered that before you'd finished deciding what you thought about it.

So — where is it. Jaw. Hands. The back of the neck. Heat across the face.

Find it. Then describe it to yourself in one word.

*Agitated.*

Now notice what that word left out. It left out them. Their name, their face, what they said, and the whole argument you've got assembled and ready.

That isn't because the argument is wrong. You may be completely right, and it will be just as right afterwards, with all of it intact.

It's because there are two things in you right now and only one of them can be worked on. The case is going nowhere. The state is here, in your hands and your jaw, and that one moves.

Now go one step deeper. Underneath the heat, something got treated as though it didn't matter. Yours, or someone's you're responsible for. Anger turns up where a line got crossed.

One layer further down, it's often one of these.

Losing any say in how it goes.

Being left to deal with it alone.

Being the problem.

Running out of time for what you still mean to do.

If one landed closer, notice which. If none did, that's fine too.

Either way, leave it where it is. Nothing to solve here.

### Regulate

Breathe in for four.

And out for six. Longer out than in, every time.

Your breath wants to go short and sharp. Let it want that. Go the other way anyway.

Your hands are closed. Open them. Fingers wide, then loose.

Attention into the middle of your chest. Not the jaw — the jaw will hold on to it. The chest.

It'll come up before it goes down. That's normal here and it's not a sign this isn't working.

Thoughts will come. Maybe a lot of them. That's what a mind does, and it doesn't mean this isn't working.

When one arrives, let it arrive. Don't push it away. Don't follow it. Don't finish it.

A thought turning up isn't a thought asking you to act.

If you notice yourself working hard at this, ease off. Watching takes no effort. Effort means you've started fighting.

Weather passes through a room. The room doesn't become the weather.

### Release

Now. The conversation in your head.

You know the one. You say the thing, they answer, you answer better. And you've run it — what, four times? Five?

Notice something about it. You win it. Every single time.

That's not you being unfair. The part of you writing that conversation is on your side — that's its entire job — so of course it hands you the better lines. It was never going to do anything else.

And every run puts your body back where it started. You've been re-lighting yourself all afternoon.

So: the anger stays. It's yours, it's earned, nobody's taking it.

Put down the rerun. Only the rerun.

And one different question, in place of the one you've been asking.

Not *what's wrong with me.*

*What was this for?*

Anger goes toward things. That's what makes it different from fear — fear backs away, this walks at it. It turns up where something's being crossed and it gets you upright to meet it.

On its own terms, it did its job.

Understanding that doesn't let anybody off. It doesn't drop a single thing you're holding.

You don't need the answer now. Keep the question. It's worth coming back to when you're steadier.

Some of this belongs to today. Some of it may be older. You don't have to sort which yet. Just notice there might be both.

One more, and it's the harder one to admit.

You might not want to put this down.

Fair enough. It works. It's the thing that stops you being walked over, and some part of you knows exactly what happens on the days you haven't got it.

So here's the smaller true thing. It was built with what you had then. What you had then and what you've got now aren't the same — you can see a state while you're standing in it, and you did that a moment ago, at the top of this.

That part of you doesn't know. Nobody told it. It's working off old information.

Nobody's asking you to stand it down. You'd be right to refuse. Only to notice that the person it's guarding isn't the one it started guarding.

Now the one that landed closest, if one did. Put it where your breath is, and leave it there.

If it was losing any say, you're right. Some of this isn't yours to control. How you go through it still is.

If it was being left alone, that fear is old, and it has its reasons. You're not deciding today who stays. That gets answered later, by what happens.

If it was being the problem, don't test it now. Picking at it from inside this state only makes it louder. Leave it where it is. And notice it didn't come from a good day.

If it was running out of time, that one's real. But nobody can look at something that size while their whole body is braced against it. Let go of the bracing. Not the fact.

None of it gets settled from inside this state. That's why it waits until you're out of it.

### Rise

Picture yourself from across the room, jaw set, running the conversation again.

*You're angry. You have a reason.*

Now picture the version of you that you'd want to be the next time this happens. Same line crossed. Same heat rising. But you're the one deciding where your attention goes.

Not on winning. On what actually matters to you here.

Where are you standing? What's your voice doing? What are the first words? Not the gist. The words.

Now step into it. The anger is still there. Your attention is where you put it.

Pick one thing you'll do this way next time.

Once that moment has happened, look back at it and write down what was different.

One more slow breath. In for four. Out for six.

Notice how you are now. Different or not, either is fine.

## T1-03 · Overwhelm Threshold

Before anything else — is there too much coming in right now? Noise, light, screens, people talking. If there is, deal with that first. Go somewhere quieter, turn something off, put headphones on. That isn't skipping this. When too much is coming in, turning the input down is the work, and everything after goes better once you have.

There's more than you can hold and nowhere obvious to put any of it. And somewhere in there is the thought that you don't have time for this. That's the state talking, and it says the same thing every time.

Nothing needs arranging. Stay where you are. Your list stays your list — sorting it is a separate job, and it goes better from the far side of this.

That's the usual order. The body goes first. The part of you that knows how to handle this comes second. It's slower, but it's there.

Most of the trouble lives in the gap between the two. The next few minutes close some of it.

Four steps. You don't need to keep track. I'll take you through them.

### Recognise

Find where it is in your body, before any of the words about it.

Chest, usually. Tightness across the top. Sometimes the throat. Sometimes a lightness in the head, or a hum through the arms with nowhere to go.

Now describe it in one word.

*Agitated.*

Notice what that word leaves out. The list. The deadline. The person waiting. The thing you forgot.

Overwhelm feels like a fact about how much there is. In the body it's a state — and the state and the volume are two different things. Related. Not the same. They come apart.

Now one sorting question. It takes a second. Too much of *what*?

Too much to do — the list, the deadlines, the people waiting.

Too much coming in — noise, light, people, screens.

Or too much at once inside you — angry and frightened and sad, all live, and no way to pick one.

No wrong answer, and some days it's all three. The reason for asking comes at the third step.

And if the honest answer is *I don't know, I just can't tell what's wrong with me today* — that is a real answer, and it's the third one. Not being able to pick is what several things at once feels like from inside.

The load is real. Nobody's telling you it isn't. It just isn't what we're working on right now.

Now go one step deeper. Underneath the volume, there's something you're afraid will drop if you stop holding all of it.

One layer further down, it's often one of these.

Losing any say in how it goes.

Being left to deal with it alone.

Being the problem.

Running out of time for what you still mean to do.

Breathe in for four.

And out for six. Longer out than in, every time.

Overwhelm breathes high and fast, up at the top of the chest. We're going the other way.

Attention into the centre of your chest. Not the list.

The list will interrupt. That's what it does. When it does, you haven't failed at this — you've noticed, which is the whole skill. Come back to the count.

Press your feet into the floor. Feel the floor push back.

There's nothing you could do about any of it in the next minute anyway.

Thoughts will come. Maybe a lot of them. That's what a mind does, and it doesn't mean this isn't working.

When one arrives, let it arrive. Don't push it away. Don't follow it. Don't finish it.

If you notice yourself working hard at this, ease off. Watching takes no effort. Effort means you've started fighting.

Weather passes through a room. The room doesn't become the weather.

### Release

There are two separate things going on, and only one of them is the load.

The first is that there's genuinely a lot. That one's real and it isn't going anywhere.

The second is something you're doing — and which one depends on your answer a moment ago.

Too much to do: you're going round the list. Checking it, then checking it again, touching everything and starting nothing. Each pass tells you what you already knew.

Too much coming in: you're pushing through. Carrying on as though the noise and the light weren't landing. Holding a normal face on. Spending everything on looking fine.

Too much at once inside you: you're sorting. Trying to work out which feeling to deal with first, as though there were an order, and getting nowhere because there isn't one.

Different in each case. The same in the way that matters — it's the expensive one, it doesn't finish, and it's the one you can put down.

Let the amount be the amount. It isn't being denied and it isn't being minimised.

Set down the scanning. Only the scanning.

And the question. Not *what's wrong with me that I can't cope with this* — that returns nothing usable, and it may not even be the right question. The volume might be genuinely unreasonable.

*What was this for?*

The state came up because something registered as more than could be met. That's a system doing its job, loudly, with no way to reduce the input.

You don't need the answer now. Keep the question. It's worth coming back to when you're steadier.

Some of this belongs to today. Some of it may be older. You don't have to sort which yet. Just notice there might be both.

Now the one that landed closest, if one did. Put it where your breath is, and leave it there.

If it was losing any say, you're right. Some of this isn't yours to control. How you go through it still is.

If it was being left alone, that fear is old, and it has its reasons. You're not deciding today who stays. That gets answered later, by what happens.

If it was running out of time, that one's real. But nobody can look at something that size while their whole body is braced against it. Let go of the bracing. Not the fact.

*You have a lot on. It is a lot. And you're steadier than when you sat down.*

Now picture the version of you that you'd want to be when the pile feels like this. Same load. Same pressure. But you're the one deciding where your attention goes.

Not on everything at once. On the next thing.

What's in front of you? What's the first movement you make? What do you let wait?
