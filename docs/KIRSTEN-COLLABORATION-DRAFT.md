## 1 · What SafeRise is

Thirty self-guided nervous-system regulation protocols across three tracks — Personal
Transformation, Relationship Healing, Professional Performance — plus a foundation
session. Each is written for a specific situation and runs the same four steps: Recognise,
Regulate, Release, Rise.

It rests on named frameworks held in three registers: peer-reviewed and load-bearing
(Porges, HeartMath, Kross & Ayduk), clinical practice (Maté), and ways of thinking named
as such (Jung, Watts). Each carries a written statement of where its evidence stops.

**It is explicitly not therapy**, makes no diagnosis, treats no condition and promises no
outcome. Its own materials repeatedly tell members that some states need another person.

Commercially: Track 01 free with an account, €19/month for the other two, workshops €39 a
head, one-to-one at €129, organisational programmes from €1,800.

## 2 · The role, as I understand it

You are **not** the clinical director, not a supervisor in the clinical-governance sense,
and not responsible for any member's care. You are not signing off on the platform as
safe and you are not being asked to lend credentials to it.

What I think you are, and what I'd like you to confirm or correct:

**A reviewer.** You read the materials that carry safety weight and tell me where they're
wrong. Not everything — the specific documents in §4.

**The person a facilitator calls.** When something happens in a live session that goes
past what the work is for, there is someone with training to speak to afterwards. Not
during — during is the emergency services or the person's own doctor.

**A check on the register.** SafeRise's credibility depends on never overclaiming. You're
better placed than I am to spot where it drifts.

### What this is not

- Not clinical responsibility for any individual member
- Not being on call
- Not attending sessions unless separately agreed
- Not supervision in the formal sense, unless you say that's what it has to be

⚠ **The most important question in this document:** does the arrangement I've described
sit within what you're professionally permitted and insured to do? If standing in on the
clinical side implies a duty of care you'd be carrying, I need to know now, because it
changes what we can sell and what we can say.

## 6 · Compensation

I don't know what's normal here and I'd rather propose something concrete and be corrected
than ask you to name a figure into a vacuum.

**Three structures. I favour the first.**

### Option A — monthly retainer

**€400 a month** for up to four hours, covering review, questions and incident
consultation. Paid whether or not the hours are used, because availability is the thing
being bought.

Anything beyond four hours at **€120 an hour**.

*Why I favour it:* predictable for both of us, doesn't make you itemise, and it prices
availability rather than output — which is what the arrangement actually is.

### Option B — hourly

**€120 an hour**, invoiced monthly against time recorded.

*Trade-off:* fairer if the work is uneven, but it means you're tracking time on something
that will often be twenty minutes of thinking, and it makes you decide whether a question
is worth billing.

### Option C — retainer plus delivery

The retainer in Option A, plus separate fees where you deliver. **See §6a — this is the
thing I'd most like an answer on.**

## 6a · Would you deliver?

This is the question I care most about, and it's separate from everything above.

**Premium one-to-one at €129 a session, and remote workshops at €39 a head.** Both are
priced, both are on the pricing page, and neither has a practitioner behind it.

### What I'd be asking

| | |
|---|---|
| **One-to-one** | 60 minutes, remote, on whatever the person is actually in. Not the protocols read aloud — those are self-guided by design. This is the thing they can't do. |
| **Workshops** | 90 minutes, remote, groups of up to 25, taking people through the work directly. |

### What you'd be paid

| | Member pays | You receive |
|---|---|---|
| One-to-one | €129 | **€85** |
| Workshop, 10 attending | €390 | **€350** |
| Workshop, 20 attending | €780 | **€450** |

One-to-one at €85 of €129 is roughly two thirds to you. Workshops are a flat fee rather
than a split, because the group size isn't something you control and you shouldn't carry
the risk of a thin room.

**These are proposals. Tell me if the split is wrong.** I've reasoned it from what the
platform charges, which may be the wrong end to start from — if €129 is under-priced for
an hour of your time, the answer is to raise the price rather than squeeze the split.

### The structural problem, which is yours to weigh

**If you deliver, you can't also be the person a facilitator calls** — not for your own
sessions. You'd be in the room. The escalation document assumes the facilitator and the
person they escalate to are different people, and that assumption breaks if you're both.

Three ways out, and I don't know which is right:

- **You deliver and someone else reviews.** Cleanest separation, most expensive, and it
  means finding a second clinician.
- **You deliver and escalate outward** — to the member's own doctor or a crisis line
  rather than to SafeRise at all. Honest, but it means SafeRise has no clinical backstop.
- **You review and don't deliver**, and delivery is Andre's or a hired facilitator's.
  Keeps the separation, and means the one-to-one offering waits.

### The time problem

The four-hour monthly ceiling in §5 is consumed entirely by review. **Delivery is
additional, not inside it.** Four one-to-ones and two workshops a month is roughly seven
hours on top, before preparation.

So this isn't a yes-or-no question — it's a question about how much of your month
SafeRise is asking for, and whether that's a sensible thing for you to give a pre-revenue
business.

### The duty-of-care question, again

§2 asks whether reviewing sits within what you're permitted and insured to do. **Delivering
is a different question with a different answer.** You'd be practising, with SafeRise as
the route by which the person found you. Whether that's your professional liability,
Kenor's, or both, is not something I can answer.

⚠ **If the answer to §6a is yes, §2's question needs a professional answer before a single
session is sold.**

## 8 · The arrangement itself

**Contractor, not employee.** Invoiced monthly, you handle your own tax and insurance.

**Term:** rolling monthly, one month's notice either way.

**Confidentiality:** mutual. You don't disclose SafeRise material; SafeRise doesn't
disclose anything about your practice.

**Intellectual property:** anything you write for SafeRise belongs to SafeRise. Anything
you bring with you remains yours. Your review comments are yours and won't be published
as endorsement.

⚠ **This needs a lawyer before it's signed, not after.** Contractor status, professional
liability and the question in §2 about duty of care all have answers I'm not qualified to
