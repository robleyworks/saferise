# SafeRise — Interim Legal Pack

**Drafted 8 September 2026 for an alpha launch with accounts and no payments.**

**I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice.** These are drafts written to
be *accurate about what SafeRise actually does*, so that they can be published
honestly now and reviewed by counsel later. The review is a real dependency —
it is deferred, not cancelled.

---

## The principle behind these drafts

**Short and true beats long and templated.**

The instinct when a lawyer isn't available is to run a policy generator. Don't.
Generators produce clauses about cookies you don't set, advertising partners you
don't have, and data you don't collect. Every one of those is a false statement
on a page whose entire purpose is accuracy, and a regulator reading a policy
that doesn't match the product will find that worse than a short policy that
does.

Your position is unusually strong here and the drafts lean into it: **the
journal never reaches you.** That is the most sensitive data on the platform and
you have designed yourself out of holding it. Say so plainly, near the top.

---

## Decisions these drafts assume

Change any of these and the text changes with it.

1. **Adults only, 18+.** Given the subject matter, the crisis exposure and the
   later intimacy track, this is the right call and it removes children's-data
   obligations and parental-consent machinery entirely.
2. **Accounts collect email and password only.** Name optional. No date of
   birth, no phone, no address, no payment details.
3. **No payments at alpha.** No card data, no billing address, no tax records.
4. **Journal and written work stay on the member's device** and are never
   transmitted.
5. **Marketing email is separate and opt-in**, with its own consent, unticked.
6. **No analytics at launch.** If you turn analytics on, the cookie section
   changes and you need a consent banner. Launching without them is cheaper and
   more honest, and you can add them deliberately later.
7. **An Article 27 EU representative is appointed** before EU residents sign up.

# 3 · Sign-up consent, verbatim

Two separate controls. The first is required, the second is not, and **neither
is pre-ticked.**

> ☐ I am 18 or over, and I agree to the Terms and the Privacy Policy.
>
> ☐ Email me when new tracks open. *(optional — you can stop this at any time)*

Do not bundle them. A single box covering both makes the marketing consent
invalid, because consent has to be freely given and separable.

# 5 · What is deliberately not in these drafts

Named so it is a decision rather than an oversight.

- **A cookie consent banner.** Nothing non-essential is set, so there is
  nothing to consent to. The moment analytics go on, this changes.
- **A refunds policy.** Nothing is sold yet.
- **The 14-day withdrawal waiver.** No purchase, so no withdrawal right to
  waive. It comes back with payments.
- **A named international transfer mechanism.** Worth asking counsel whether
  one is needed at all: data given directly by a person to a controller outside
  the EU may not be a Chapter V transfer. Not a launch blocker either way, but
  it is the first question to put to a lawyer.
- **A Data Protection Officer.** Not required at this scale and this data.
- **Special-category data handling.** Not needed, because the journal never
  reaches you. This is only true while that stays true — the moment anything a
  member writes is synced to a server, this whole pack needs rewriting.

# 6 · What has to happen before EU residents sign up

1. Appoint the Article 27 representative and put their details on the page.
2. Create the mailbox and check it receives.
3. Fill every placeholder from the providers' own documentation.
4. Publish all three documents with working links, not `href="#"`.
5. Verify every crisis resource against its provider's current page.

Then book the counsel review. Publishing first and reviewing after is a real
risk you are choosing to take; taking it knowingly with an accurate document is
a much smaller risk than taking it unknowingly with a generated one.
